## 1. Scope and boundary

- Exterior cleaning of photovoltaic (PV) modules on roofs and ground mounts, using water and approved low-pressure methods, with owner authority and no electrical work.
- Prior research defines these areas: client permissions and system ownership; electrical hazards and live-generation assumptions; ground-based alternatives; fragile roofs; module damage and exposed wiring; weather and heat; manufacturer and warranty instructions; water application and equipment separation; vegetation and biological hazards; ground protection and runoff; defects and referrals; job records (PRIOR).
- Out of scope: electrical inspection, system servicing, switching or isolation, roof access approval, warranty certification and energy-output testing (PRIOR research note).
- Out of scope: battery work. Battery hazards are noted by Energy Safe Victoria, but battery service is not part of cleaning.

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- Installation and safety requirements for PV arrays: AS/NZS 5033 (LISTED). Store listing shows the 2021 edition; earlier 2012 and 2014 editions also appear. Current edition and cleaning-related clauses: UNCONFIRMED from the primary text (fetch refused this pass).
- Safe working on low-voltage installations: AS/NZS 4836 (named by Energy Safe Victoria, READ).
- Wiring rules: AS/NZS 3000 (named by Energy Safe Victoria, READ).
- Energy Safe Victoria guidance (READ):
  - DC supply cables from the array can still be live even when the power is off. Avoid contact with them.
  - Isolate by turning off and locking out and tagging out all electricity supplied to the property at the main switchboard before any work.
  - Installation work is for licensed electrical workers. Fall prevention on roofs, battery hazards and heat-related illness are covered.
- Installer accreditation: Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA) took over the accreditation scheme from the Clean Energy Council on 29 February 2024, and the CEC has ceased its accreditation services (READ). Do not describe CEC accreditation as current.
- Solar Victoria maintenance guide (PRIOR; listed; not read). Commonwealth energy guidance "Look after your solar system" (PRIOR; not read).
- Water quality and mineral deposits: manufacturer and warranty instructions. Specific guidance: UNCONFIRMED.
- Manufacturer and warranty conditions: must be checked for each make and model before any method is approved (PRIOR research note). Specific conditions: UNCONFIRMED.
- Chemicals: SDS and labels. The Safe Work Australia SDS page is PRIOR only.

## 3. Main hazards

- Falls from roofs and ladders (SafeWork NSW construction page READ; WorkSafe Victoria working-at-heights resource for solar installations, PRIOR).
- DC electrical hazard: arrays can remain live while light is present, and DC cables can stay live when mains is off (READ).
- Water on electrical components, connectors and inverters. Keep water off electrical connections (PRIOR).
- Damaged modules and exposed wiring: stop and report.
- Fragile roofs and skylights (WorkSafe Victoria, PRIOR; SafeWork SA, LISTED).
- Heat and fatigue on roofs (READ, Energy Safe Victoria summary).
- Biological hazards: nests, bird droppings, vegetation.
- Runoff and chemical contamination of stormwater.

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- Cleaning only. No electrical work, switching, isolation, inverter or battery work, or wiring checks.
- Do not treat an inverter switch-off as making the array safe. The prior research states this and it is consistent with the Energy Safe Victoria cable warning (READ). The point that switching off an inverter does not de-energise an array is PRIOR and not confirmed against a primary Australian source this pass: UNCONFIRMED as stated.
- No roof access without approved fall protection, a competent person and a ground-based alternative check.
- Do not make energy-output, warranty, or system-condition claims.
- Completing the course does not grant any electrical licence, accreditation or certification.

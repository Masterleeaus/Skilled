## 1. Scope and boundaries

In scope:
- Inspection of sprinkler heads, drip lines, emitters, valves, filters, rain sensors and controllers that are already installed.
- Zone testing (run each zone, check coverage and flow), head alignment, and nozzle changes.
- Controller programming and seasonal schedule changes, within the controller's manual.
- Visual leak detection and reporting. Repairs to pipework on the non-plumbing side of a backflow device are UNCONFIRMED per state (section 5).
- Water-use records and client advice on schedules.

Out of scope or referred:
- Connecting to the water supply, installing or testing backflow prevention devices, and any work on pressurised zones (licensed plumber or registered practitioner; section 5).
- Excavation to repair underground pipes. Use Before You Dig Australia first (free referral, read; no date).
- Wiring of controllers, solenoid valves and mains supply (licensed electrician; section 5).
- Agricultural irrigation (outside the VIC non-agricultural class; VBA page, read).
- Working within local water restriction rules without checking them (state rules UNCONFIRMED in this pass).

## 2. Key technical methods

- Pre-service check: isolate the controller before opening valve boxes; confirm the master valve and the pump or meter state where present.
- Zone test: run each zone for a set time; check each head's spray pattern, drip emitter output and any leaks; note the zone's water pressure at the test point.
- Head adjustment: align heads with the path and garden bed edges; avoid spraying paths and walls.
- Filter and strainer cleaning: remove, rinse and refit; check seals.
- Controller programming: set run times, days and start times to the plant needs and local rules; record each change.
- Backflow device visual check: confirm the device is present and not damaged, and refer testing to the licensed or registered practitioner.
- Leak check: check for wet spots, sunken soil, and meter reading changes; record the location and refer.
- Records: zone map, run times, faults, parts replaced, and client sign-off.

## 4. Main hazards

- Cross-contamination of potable water through incorrect connections or missing backflow devices.
- Electric shock at controllers, valve boxes, pumps and wet terminals.
- Excavation strikes on underground services. Use Before You Dig Australia before any dig.
- Slips and falls at valve boxes, wet paths and steps.
- Traffic and footpath hazards when working near roads.
- Manual handling of heavy valve boxes, lids, and bags of materials.
- Chemical exposure if herbicides or fertilisers are used (see `lawn.md` section 5).
- Sun, heat and dehydration.

## 5. Regulator references and licence facts

Plumbing and backflow:

- [NSW-06, CONFIRMED] Plumbing, draining and gasfitting need a licence or certificate regardless of cost. The named categories include "Water plumbing", with "urban irrigation" named as part of it (NSW Building Commission page, last updated 31 July 2026). Irrigation work outside urban settings is not addressed in the page read.
- [VIC, Irrigation (non-agricultural), VBA, no date, read] Registration class under the Plumbing Regulations 2018, Part 4. Covers practitioners who carry out non-agricultural irrigation work on a property, including connected design work. The page says the regulated work runs from the connection point to the last valve controlling any pressurised zone. Compliance references include AS/NZS 2845 and AS/NZS 3500.1. Qualification routes are competency plus recent experience, or Certificate II or higher plus two years of supervised experience under a licensed plumber.
- [QLD-06, CONFIRMED] Plumbing and drainage need a QBCC licence at any value. Whether irrigation sits within the plumbing class is UNCONFIRMED in the matrix.
- [SA-18, PARTIAL] SA plumbing contractor licence covers water or sanitary plumbing, drainage, and backflow prevention devices. Licence trigger value not stated (page last updated 19 October 2018).
- [ACT-18, PARTIAL] ACT plumber classes include "irrigation" as a named class. Scope of each class not stated on the page read.
- [NT-17, CONFIRMED] NT plumbing requires both water supply and sanitary work for the plumbing class; irrigation not named (UNCONFIRMED for irrigation).
- [TAS-16, PARTIAL] Owner minor repair exemption (Occupational Licensing (Plumbing Exemptions) Order 2015). Not a rule for irrigation contractors.
- [WA-24, UNCONFIRMED] WA plumbing licence page not retrieved in this pass.

Electrical controllers and valves:

- [NSW-07, CONFIRMED] Electrical wiring needs an electrical contractor licence holder or certificate regardless of cost. The page does not mention irrigation controllers by name.
- [QLD-07, PARTIAL] Electrical mechanic licence rules named for energy systems; controllers not named.
- [VIC-33, PARTIAL] Electrical licence and registration routes; scope of each licence not stated.
- Whether low-voltage controller wiring needs an electrical licence in each state is UNCONFIRMED.

Excavation:

- Before You Dig Australia (read; no date). It is the new name for Dial Before You Dig and remains a free service. Requests are made through an online portal.

Water restrictions:

- State and council restrictions are UNCONFIRMED in this pass. Do not state a restriction rule in the lesson without a named official source.

## 6. Scope points for licence notices

- The lesson does not authorise plumbing, backflow installation or testing, or work on pressurised zones. The notice should say which licence covers the work in the state of the lesson, and that the lesson does not grant it.
- The lesson does not authorise electrical work on controllers or valves.
- NSW wording: water plumbing (including urban irrigation) is a named licence category [NSW-06]. VIC wording: the non-agricultural irrigation class is a named registration class (VBA page). Use state-specific notices only.

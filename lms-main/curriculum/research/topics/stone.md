# Research brief: Stone and terrazzo restoration

- Course slug: `stone`
- Group: Restoration (added as a business; DECISIONS.md leaves the grouping to the architect)

- Brief date: 2026-11-02

- Source key and UNCONFIRMED as defined in residential.md. Note: the architect may group this with timber and furniture restoration (DECISIONS.md).

## 1. Scope and boundary

- Cleaning, polishing, honing, sealing and surface restoration of natural stone (marble, granite, limestone, slate) and terrazzo floors, benchtops and feature surfaces.
- Out of scope: any work on engineered (manufactured) stone, which is subject to the national ban on processing and installation (see section 2). Structural repair, cutting and installation: out of scope.
- Tile and grout sealing sits in the tiling business (DECISIONS.md), not here.

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- Engineered stone (reconstituted or quartz-based surfaces): national ban on manufacture, supply, processing and installation from 1 July 2024 (READ, SA Government page). The page states that installed engineered stone does not have to be removed and can be used safely while undisturbed. Work that removes, repairs or alters it should not be done because it can release silica dust (READ). Whether the ban covers imports: UNCONFIRMED (the page does not list imports).
- Silica dust from grinding, honing and polishing natural stone and terrazzo: the Safe Work Australia guidance on working with crystalline silica substances is PRIOR only (fetch refused this pass). Prior research: controls are suppression and extraction, isolation, respiratory protection, and a wet-method runoff assessment. Australian state law on silica exposure: UNCONFIRMED.
- Respirators: AS/NZS 1716 is named by WorkSafe Victoria for respirator equipment (READ). Fit testing and respirator selection for stone work: UNCONFIRMED.
- Vacuum and extraction: prior research says standard domestic vacuums are unsuitable for hazardous dust, and HEPA alone does not establish hazard-class approval. The NZ WorkSafe page is PRIOR. Australian equivalent: UNCONFIRMED.
- Chemical selection (acid etches, stone cleaners, sealers): SDS duties. The Safe Work Australia SDS page is PRIOR only.
- Electrical equipment (polishers, wet saws): AS/NZS 3760 (LISTED).
- Surface method (honing grits, polishing, sealing): manufacturer instructions. No Australian standard confirmed. UNCONFIRMED.

## 3. Main hazards

- Respirable crystalline silica from grinding, honing, polishing and dry processes.
- Acid and etching chemicals: burns and fumes.
- Slips from slurry and wet polishing residue.
- Electrical hazard from wet polishers and saws.
- Manual handling of heavy slabs and equipment.
- Stone slurry and runoff to drains.
- Unknown engineered stone mistaken for natural stone: stop and check, because the ban applies.

- Victoria: WorkSafe Victoria (READ on general hazards).
- Queensland, WA and other states: UNCONFIRMED for silica rules.
- Wastewater from wet processes: state environment authorities. UNCONFIRMED per state.

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- Do not grind, hone, cut or alter engineered stone. Stop and report.
- Do not dry-grind, dry sweep or use compressed air on silica-containing dust.
- Do not use acid etchers near people or pets without a documented control plan.
- No structural repair, cutting or installation.
- Completing the course does not grant silica, engineered stone or any licence.

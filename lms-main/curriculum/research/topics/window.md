## 1. Scope and boundary

- Glass cleaning of residential and commercial windows and facades at any height, from ground level and on access equipment, under the supervised and authorised-only track.
- Prior research defines these lesson areas: job authority and scope; avoiding work at height; facade and glazing risk screening; public protection and falling objects; weather; water-fed poles and ground-based work; ladders and platforms; rope access and anchor certification; elevated work platforms and competence; emergency and rescue; chemicals and runoff; inspection evidence and handover (PRIOR).
- Out of scope unless specifically authorised and trained: rope access, suspended platforms, and elevated work platforms. Their Australian certification requirements are UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
- Out of scope: glazing repair, sealant or frame work, and structural inspection of facades.

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- Work-at-height hierarchy (READ, SafeWork NSW construction page): eliminate the need for height; use fall-prevention devices such as temporary platforms, edge protection or scaffolding; use work-positioning systems; use fall-arrest systems only if prevention and positioning are not possible. Where a fall could exceed two metres, the page states that a safe work method statement is required.
- Ladders (READ, same page): do not stand on the top two rungs or overreach; extension ladders should extend at least one metre past the landing.
- Scaffolds (READ, same page): check foundations, bracing, tying, guardrails and the inspection tag; tradespeople without a scaffold licence must not erect, alter or dismantle a scaffold where the fall or object-drop risk exceeds four metres (per the page).
- Falls prevention code: Safe Work Australia Model Code of Practice: Managing the risk of falls at workplaces (READ, model status; legal effect by jurisdiction).
- Rope access and fall arrest: AS/NZS 1891.4 (industrial fall-arrest systems and devices, Part 4: selection, use and maintenance). LISTED; editions 2009 and 2025 appear in store listings. Whether it sets the rope-access requirement: UNCONFIRMED.
- Water-fed pole systems and ground-based cleaning: prior research lists these as ground-level alternatives (PRIOR). Specific Australian standard: UNCONFIRMED.
- Electrical risk near overhead power lines when using water-fed poles or extended tools: UNCONFIRMED Australian reference this pass. Check against state electrical safety rules.
- Chemicals and runoff: prior research. NSW EPA stormwater page (PRIOR; not re-read).
- Elevated work platforms: Australian standard not confirmed this pass. UNCONFIRMED.

## 3. Main hazards

- Falls from height (the leading hazard). Ladders, platforms, rope systems and roof edges.
- Falling objects (tools, cleaning heads, water-fed poles) onto the public and workers below. Exclusion zones.
- Glass breakage and facade damage.
- Wind and weather. Stop-work triggers.
- Electrical contact with overhead lines when using long conductive poles (UNCONFIRMED Australian reference).
- Chemicals and runoff onto footpaths and drains.
- Manual handling of heavy platforms and equipment.
- Rescue after a fall: must be planned in advance (PRIOR research; state rules UNCONFIRMED).

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- Ground-based and low-level work is the default. Height work requires approved access equipment, a documented plan, and a competent supervisor.
- No rope-access or suspended-platform work without specialist certification, which the course does not grant (UNCONFIRMED requirement by state).
- No work above the trained height limit, in unsafe weather, or near energised lines.
- Do not certify facade or glazing condition. Report defects in writing.
- Completing the course does not grant any licence, scaffold or height-work authority.

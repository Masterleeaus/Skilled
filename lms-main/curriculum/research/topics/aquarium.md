## 1. Scope and boundaries

In scope:
- Tank cleaning (glass, substrate and décor), partial water changes, and filter maintenance.
- Water testing and records (freshwater parameters and marine parameters, kept apart).
- Equipment checks: heaters, pumps, lights, skimmers, air lines, and controllers (visual and power-off checks only).
- Maintenance of plants and décor, with records of changes.
- Client advice on stocking limits, feeding and biosecurity, without selling animals.

Out of scope or referred:
- Buying, selling, moving or importing fish, plants or live rock. These are restricted by state and national rules (section 5). Do not use the course to train people in the trade.
- Collection of marine livestock from the wild: UNCONFIRMED in this pass; treat as out of scope.
- Animal health treatment and medication: refer to a veterinarian or an aquatic health specialist. UNCONFIRMED which professions may prescribe or dose.
- Electrical work (refer to a licensed electrician).
- Plumbing or drainage connections (refer to a licensed plumber).
- Releasing animals or water to the environment.

## 2. Key technical methods

- Water testing: ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH, temperature; for marine tanks add salinity or specific gravity and any additional parameters the client needs. Target ranges UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
- Partial water change: match temperature and salinity; treat tap water for chlorine and chloramine before use (method is a technique point; product choice per label).
- Filter maintenance: rinse media in removed tank water, not tap water; keep the biological media wet and avoid replacing all media at once. Technique point, source UNCONFIRMED.
- Glass and substrate cleaning: use tools that do not scratch; vacuum substrate in sections to limit stress.
- Equipment checks: check heater settings and cords, pump impellers, air lines and light timers with the power off or the RCD engaged, as the equipment instructions say.
- Records: test results, water changes, equipment faults and client decisions.

## 4. Main hazards

- Electric shock: heaters, pumps, lights and controllers near water. Use safety switches and check cords and seals.
- Glass tank breakage or leak: UNCONFIRMED standard for tank loads and glass. Avoid lifting or moving full tanks without a safe method.
- Chemical hazards: water conditioners, salt mixes, medications, and cleaning products. Read labels and keep them with the product.
- Slips and water damage from spills.
- Manual handling of heavy tanks, cabinets, stands and bags of substrate.
- Biosecurity: moving water, plants or fish between tanks or sites; disease transfer. Quarantine rules UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
- Animal welfare: stress from handling and cleaning; keep fish out of the air or bright light for long periods (technique point).

## 5. Regulator references and licence facts

Licence facts: no aquarium rows in the matrices. Points below come from regulator pages and are not matrix rows.

- NSW (DPI aquarium page, undated, read): Prohibited and notifiable species cannot be possessed, bought, sold or moved (Schedule 2, Biosecurity Act; Schedule 1, Biosecurity Regulation). The page says buying fish, plants or live rock from overseas online is illegal. Releasing fish into NSW public waters needs a fish stocking permit. Dumping is an offence under the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act 1979.
- QLD (invasive fish page, updated 28 May 2026, read): Prohibited high-risk aquarium species cannot be possessed or brought in; if an owner suspects one, contact Biosecurity Queensland immediately. Fish not on the Live Import List are biosecurity matter; importing or trading them is an offence under the EPBC Act. Owners cannot keep offspring of fish bred in Australia before import restrictions applied to that species.
- WA, SA, VIC, TAS, ACT, NT: UNCONFIRMED in this pass. WA (fish.wa.gov.au) search result only; not opened.
- Electrical: as in `pond.md` section 5. [NSW-07, CONFIRMED] for wiring work; [QLD-07, PARTIAL].
- Animal sales and pet-shop licences: UNCONFIRMED in this pass. Do not state that a licence is or is not needed to maintain aquariums for clients.

## 1. Scope and boundary

- Cleaning and maintenance of textile floor coverings (carpet) and upholstered furniture in homes and commercial premises.
- Out of scope: carpet installation and repair, upholstery repair and re-covering, flood or biohazard contamination, and mould remediation (High-risk module).
- Mattress cleaning is residential only (DECISIONS.md) and is covered in the residential brief.

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- Carpet maintenance standard: AS/NZS 3733 ("The cleaning maintenance of residential and commercial carpeting"). READ via trade press (In Clean magazine, undated). The article says the standard was first prepared in 1995, that a revision is underway, and that a revised draft was open for public comment with submissions due on 4 September. The year of the draft and the current published edition: UNCONFIRMED.
- Upholstery cleaning standards in Australia: none confirmed. UNCONFIRMED.
- Extraction and hot-water methods, dry and low-moisture methods, and encapsulation: manufacturer and industry practice. No Australian standard confirmed for each method. UNCONFIRMED.
- Chemical selection and dilution: manufacturer instructions and SDS. The Safe Work Australia SDS page is PRIOR only.
- Electrical equipment (extractors, vacuums, cords): AS/NZS 3760 (LISTED). Its applicability to a contractor's equipment: UNCONFIRMED.
- Fabric colour-fastness and test patches: general practice. UNCONFIRMED as a standard.
- Hygiene and dust control on carpets: the wet-cleaning approach is widely used for dust control. Dry sweeping of dust-containing surfaces is a dust-generating activity (WorkSafe Victoria timber guidance, READ, lists dry sweeping as dust-generating). Apply the same logic to crystalline-silica dust only where a site assessment says so (see construction brief).

## 3. Main hazards

- Chemical exposure from cleaning solutions, pre-sprays, and sanitising products. Ventilation and SDS required.
- Slips on wet carpets and in hallways. Post-clean drying time is a slip and fall risk.
- Electrical risk from wet extraction equipment and cords (AS/NZS 3760, LISTED).
- Manual handling of extraction machines, furniture moving and stair work.
- Falls on stairs and when working on upholstery at height: UNCONFIRMED.
- Unrecognised contamination (biological, drug, flood-borne). Stop and refer. Do not clean over it.
- Cross-contamination between jobs (equipment and solution): UNCONFIRMED standard.

## 4. Regulator references

- Work health and safety: Safe Work Australia model laws, state regulators as in residential brief.
- Electrical equipment: state electrical safety regulators (e.g., Energy Safe Victoria, READ for PV only). In-service testing requirements by state: UNCONFIRMED.
- Consumer claims (e.g., "removes all stains" or "allergy-free"): Australian Consumer Law and ACCC guidance. Not read this pass. UNCONFIRMED.
- Wastewater from carpet extraction: state environmental authority and local stormwater and sewer rules. UNCONFIRMED as to specific rules per state (see the car detailing and pressure brief for wastewater principles, PRIOR).

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- Do not represent a carpet clean as removing all stains, mould or allergens without evidence.
- No carpet or upholstery repair, re-stretching or re-fixing unless separately trained and licensed where needed.
- Stop and refer where mould, biological contamination, flood damage or drug residue is suspected.
- Do not dispose of wastewater to stormwater.

## 1. Scope and boundaries

In scope (maintenance):
- Inspection of timber decks, fences and gates: rot, fastener corrosion, loose boards, rails and posts, and gate hardware.
- Cleaning, sanding, stain and sealer application to manufacturer instructions.
- Replacement of boards, palings, handrail sections, and small fittings, where the structure is sound and the fixing points are unchanged.
- Hardware adjustment: gate self-closing and latch on pool and boundary gates (checked against the pool rules in `pool.md`).
- Client advice and referral when structure is unsafe.

Out of scope or referred:
- New deck or fence construction, and any work that alters the footprint, height or structure (licence and permit rules in section 5).
- Structural repairs to bearers, joists, posts in the ground, or ledgers (refer to a licensed builder or engineer).
- Balustrade compliance certification and height checks against the building code (refer to a building surveyor or the state authority).
- Pool barrier certification (see `pool.md`).
- Termite inspection or treatment (licensed pest work; UNCONFIRMED in this pass).
- Removal of asbestos-sheet fencing (licensed removalist; section 5).

## 2. Key technical methods

- Inspection: probe timber for soft or rotted areas; check ledger fixings and post bases; check handrail strength by hand; record photos.
- Treated timber: cut ends and drilled holes need the field treatment named by the treated-timber standard (AS 1604 listing; treatment details UNCONFIRMED).
- Cleaning: use the lowest pressure that cleans, and keep the nozzle moving; power washing can damage timber surfaces and must be done within the tool's limits.
- Sanding and stain: follow the product label; test a small area; allow the stated dry time before rain or re-coating.
- Board replacement: remove fixings without splitting adjacent boards; use corrosion-resistant fixings suited to the timber and the environment; set gaps for timber movement.
- Fence repair: check post stability by hand; replace palings with the same fixing method; keep the fence line.
- Hardware: check hinge alignment and gate closers; adjust so the gate closes and latches from any position where it is a pool gate.
- Records: condition report, timber species if known, products used, photos and client sign-off.

## 4. Main hazards

- Falls from decks, fence tops and ladders; collapse of rotten timber.
- Manual handling of boards, sheets and posts.
- Powered tools: saw and sander injuries, dust, and noise.
- Power washer hazards, including hose whip, kickback at the nozzle and electrical hazards near outlets and water.
- Chemicals: timber treatments, strippers and solvents (SDS and labels).
- Asbestos in older fibro fencing and cladding (section 5).
- Lead in old paint on timber and fences (AS/NZS 4361.2 title).
- Timber dust and splinters.
- Termite damage concealed in timber (identification UNCONFIRMED in this pass).

## 5. Regulator references and licence facts

NSW:
- [NSW-04, PARTIAL] Contractor licence or qualified supervisor certificate for work over $5,000 (labour and materials, including GST). Timber decks and timber and metal fencing are named only as examples in the footing and structural concreting context. Whole deck or fence construction is not confirmed to be carpentry under this row.
- [NSW-20, PARTIAL] Exempt development rules (planning, not licensing): decks maximum 3 m above existing ground, floor no more than 1 m above ground, setback and area limits (25 m2) per the Planning Portal page (updated 23 March 2026). Residential fences: 1.8 m side and rear (1.2 m masonry), road frontage 1.2 m, in residential zones R1 to R4 and RU5 (updated 20 June 2023). Planning exemption does not change the $5,000 licence threshold.
- [NSW-21, CONFIRMED] Pool barrier and gate rules where a pool fence is involved.

QLD:
- [QLD-13, PARTIAL] Fence licences: Carpentry for timber and metal fences; licence not needed if the fence value is under $3,300 on non-agricultural land (factsheet; undated, code suggests 2019 per matrix). Deck licence class not found.
- [QLD-16, UNCONFIRMED] State deck and fence planning rules not found; councils and the Queensland Development Code set them (not fetched). Some fences need building approval; check the council before building a fence higher than 2 m (QBCC pool-fence page).
- [QLD-15, CONFIRMED] Pool barrier rules where a pool is present.

VIC:
- [VIC-10, CONFIRMED] Attached decks need a building permit regardless of size; detached decks forming part of the amenity also need one (VBA practice note BP-01).
- [VIC-12, CONFIRMED] Fences up to 2 m are exempt from permit; within 3 m of a street, non-masonry fences up to 1.5 m and masonry up to 1.2 m; within 9 m of an intersection, up to 1 m above footpath.
- [VIC-11, PARTIAL] Deck construction contract may need registration if over $10,000 (inference from the exemption list; not confirmed).
- [VIC-13, PARTIAL] Fence construction contract may need registration if over $10,000 (inference).

WA:
- [WA-10, PARTIAL] Timber decking and fences are listed on the builder checklist as work that does not require builder registration (checklist undated).
- [WA-11, UNCONFIRMED] Permits for decks and fences not checked; confirm with the local government permit authority.

SA:

## 6. Scope points for licence notices

- NSW: the $5,000 contractor licence threshold applies to carpentry; the planning exemption limits do not change it.
- QLD: fence licence class and $3,300 threshold apply to fences; deck licensing is not confirmed.
- VIC: attached decks always need a permit; the lesson does not state permit exemptions for decks.
- The lesson does not grant any licence or permit.

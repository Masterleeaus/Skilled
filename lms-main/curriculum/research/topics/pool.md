## 1. Scope and boundaries

In scope (lesson-level work):
- Routine cleaning of pool and spa surfaces, skimmer baskets, pump strainers and waterline.
- Water sampling, test-kit reading, and recording of results.
- Chemical dosing, with separation and labelling rules.
- Filter cleaning and backwash, pump and equipment checks.
- Visual check of safety barriers and gates, and the owner's duty to keep them working.
- Handover, periodic-care records, and client advice.

Out of scope or referred:
- Electrical work (bonding, RCD, wiring, pump and light connections). Refer to a licensed electrician.
- Gas heater installation or repair. Refer to a licensed gasfitter (see section 6).
- Barrier certification. In NSW, QLD, VIC and WA the certifying role sits with specified inspectors or certifiers, not with a service technician (see section 5).
- Structural pool work, liner or tile repair beyond cosmetic care, drain or outlet modification.
- Biological contamination and incidents: escalate, do not remediate (see `pool-cleaning-L09` source lesson).

## 2. Key technical methods

- Pre-start inspection: check barrier, gate latch, signage, equipment condition, chemical stock and labels, and the chemical separation area before any dosing.
- Water testing: sample at a set depth and location; record free chlorine, pH, total alkalinity, and calcium hardness with the test method used. Target ranges are UNCONFIRMED in this pass. Use the manufacturer's test-kit instructions and the regulator or standard the client's jurisdiction names.
- Chemical dosing: dose one product at a time, with the pump running and the return flowing; keep incompatible products apart (see section 4). Dosing order and quantities are UNCONFIRMED as a national rule.
- Circulation and filtration: check skimmer and main-drain outlets, return jets, pump strainer, and filter pressure. Outlet and cover checks link to AS 1926.3 (see section 3).
- Backwash and filter service: follow the filter manufacturer's procedure; record pressure before and after.
- Barrier check: gate closes and latches from any position; barrier height and gap are checked against the state rule (see section 5). A check is not a certificate.
- Records: test results, chemicals used, equipment condition, defects, and client advice in writing.

## 4. Main hazards

- Drowning and entrapment at outlets and suction points (AS 1926.3 link).
- Toxic gas: sodium or calcium hypochlorite mixed with acids, or exposed to heat or moisture, can release chlorine gas. Chlorine gas is heavier than air and builds up in poorly ventilated spaces (WorkSafe WA, read; last updated 17 Dec 2025).
- Fire and explosion risk from oxidisers in contact with heat, moisture or incompatible chemicals.
- Corrosive and oxidising product contact (eyes, skin, respiratory).
- Electric shock from submersible or mains equipment; a very low voltage can be hazardous in water (WorkSafe NT bulletin, read; no date). Equipment must be bonded and protected as the bulletin describes.
- Falls from ladders, pool edges and wet surfaces; slips.
- Manual handling of chemical drums and filter media.
- Biological contamination (incident escalation only; no remediation in this course).

## 6. Scope points for licence notices

- A barrier check or service visit does not make the technician a barrier inspector or certifier. Use the state's inspector role.
- Electrical bonding and pump connections are licensed electrical work (NSW-07 for wiring, any cost; QLD electrical rules; VIC-33 electrical licence). The lesson does not authorise this work.
- Gas heaters: out of scope. Gasfitting rules are in the boundaries section of `handyman` and in the matrices (NSW-06, QLD-06, VIC-27).
- Do not state that a pool chemical licence is or is not needed in any state. Use "UNCONFIRMED" wording.

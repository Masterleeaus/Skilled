## 1. Scope and boundary

- Routine exterior pressure cleaning of surfaces such as driveways, paths, walls, decks and outdoor equipment, at low and standard pressures, at ground level and where access is approved.
- Industrial water jetting, hazardous remediation and specialist high-pressure work are out of scope and need independent training and authorisation (PRIOR scope statement).
- Mould-related content in pressure-cleaning lesson 09 is kept with full content and warnings (DECISIONS.md). That content must not be read as authority to remove mould.
- Roof work is in the roof soft washing course. Window work is in the window course.

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- High-pressure water jetting equipment and operators: AS/NZS 4233.1, named by WorkSafe Victoria as the reference for jetting controls (READ, safety alert). Edition and scope: UNCONFIRMED (the standard text was not read).
- Equipment class and operator competency (READ, WorkSafe Victoria alert, which relies on AS/NZS 4233.1):
  - Equipment must not be modified or fitted with attachments without written approval from the manufacturer or a competent person. Class B equipment changes need a competent person with suitable engineering skills.
  - Only the manufacturer, its agent or a competent person should repair or maintain the equipment.
  - All parts, fittings and hoses should be compatible, correctly sized and rated at or above the equipment's maximum operating pressure.
  - Class B operators must be trained and assessed as competent through a registered training organisation. Trainers must be RTO-accredited and have at least five years' relevant experience. Refresher training must be recorded at least every two years, and whenever new or more powerful equipment is introduced.
- PPE (READ, same alert): eye protection is required near jetting; the operator in control of the water flow wears at least safety glasses and a face shield; head protection, body and leg armour where risk requires, and hearing protection where noise cannot be controlled.
- Guide: Safe Work Australia Guide for managing risks from high-pressure water jetting (LISTED; the page refused a direct fetch on robots grounds). Content not read.
- Competency unit: MSMWJ202 "Use high pressure water jetting equipment" (WA Department of Training listing, PRIOR; not read). Other competency units appear in search results (LISTED).
- Electrical equipment: AS/NZS 3760 (LISTED).
- Wastewater: state stormwater and sewer rules. NSW EPA stormwater guidance (PRIOR; not re-read).
- Chemicals: SDS and label duties. The Safe Work Australia SDS page is PRIOR only.
- Routine pressure settings and surface-specific pressures: UNCONFIRMED. Do not teach numerical pressure values without a source.

## 3. Main hazards

- High-pressure injection injury. A small entry wound can hide deep tissue damage (READ, WorkSafe Victoria alert, which describes the water jet piercing skin and injecting contaminated material).
- Flying debris and eye injury (READ).
- Flailing hose, uncontrolled nozzle, or a separated hose coupling striking a person (READ).
- Noise, confined spaces, falls, hazardous chemicals or biological material, and electric shock (READ).
- Slips from wash water and runoff.
- Electrical risk from water near power points and outdoor fittings (overseas consumer guidance, PRIOR; Australian reference UNCONFIRMED).
- Chemical exposure from detergents and mould or algae cleaners.

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- Routine exterior cleaning only. Industrial water jetting, hazardous remediation and Class B equipment work require RTO-assessed competency. This course does not grant that competency.
- Do not modify equipment, fit non-approved attachments or repair hoses and couplings.
- Do not clean mould, remediate contaminated surfaces or treat asbestos-containing materials.
- Do not discharge wash water to stormwater.
- Completing the course does not grant any licence, certificate of competency or authority to operate industrial jetting equipment.

## 1. Scope and boundaries

In scope:
- Site assessment: levels, fall to drainage, soil type, sun and shade, existing services, and client brief.
- Soft landscaping: soil preparation, planting, mulching, establishment watering advice, and garden bed edging.
- Low-level hardscape, such as edging and small paths, where the base and fall are simple and no structural element is involved.
- Coordination with irrigation, turf and drainage contractors.
- Scope, approval, variations, and records.

Out of scope or referred:
- Retaining walls above the state permit threshold, and any wall that retains load or supports structure (refer to an engineer; permit rules in section 5).
- Stormwater and drainage connections (licensed plumber or drainer).
- Excavation near services without a Before You Dig Australia referral (free service, read; no date).
- Concrete and structural works (UNCONFIRMED rules per state in this pass).
- Electrical lighting and power (licensed electrician).
- Tree removal and tree work at height (see `garden.md`).
- Pesticide application for hire (see `lawn.md` section 5).

## 2. Key technical methods

- Site survey: measure levels and falls; mark services; photograph existing conditions before work.
- Soil assessment: test or observe texture and drainage; use soil products to the soil standard named below.
- Soil preparation: cultivate to the depth the plants need; add amendments by test result, not by guess (rates UNCONFIRMED in this pass).
- Planting: set plants at the correct depth; water in; apply mulch to the depth stated in the design (UNCONFIRMED numeric values in this pass).
- Edging and paving base: compaction and base depth depend on the material and use; use the manufacturer's specification and any named standard once confirmed.
- Retaining walls: identify the wall height, surcharge, drainage and whether a permit applies; refer the structural design.
- Waste and materials: sort soil, rock and green waste; take to an approved facility.
- Records: site survey, plant list, materials used, photos before and after, and approvals.

## 4. Main hazards

- Underground and overhead services (excavation strike, shock, gas or water release).
- Collapse of excavations and retaining structures.
- Crush injuries from rocks, pavers and wall units.
- Manual handling of heavy materials.
- Silica dust from cutting pavers or stone (exposure limits not checked in this pass; treat as UNCONFIRMED).
- Wet concrete or mortar burns (UNCONFIRMED standard or guidance).
- Chemical hazards: fertilisers, herbicides, adhesives and sealers.
- Biosecurity: moving soil, mulch and turf in fire ant zones (see `lawn.md` section 5).
- Asbestos in fill or old structures.

## 5. Regulator references and licence facts

Retaining walls and structures:

- [VIC-12, CONFIRMED] Fence and wall height rules in the building permit practice note (BP-01). Retaining walls under 1 m are exempt unless part of other building work or protecting adjoining property.
- [NT-05, PARTIAL] Retaining walls over 600 mm need a permit in building control areas (NT page, published 29 January 2024).
- Other states: UNCONFIRMED in this pass. Do not state thresholds for NSW, QLD, WA, SA, TAS or ACT.

Drainage and plumbing:

- [NSW-06, CONFIRMED] Plumbing and draining need a licence or certificate regardless of cost, with named categories including draining work.
- [QLD-06, CONFIRMED] QBCC licence for plumbing or drainage at any value.
- [VIC-30, PARTIAL] Drainage plumbing class is named by the BPC; statement that licence is required for all such work not quoted.
- [SA-18, PARTIAL] SA plumbing contractor licence covers drainage.
- [ACT-18, PARTIAL] ACT drainer class; scope not stated.
- [NT-17, CONFIRMED] NT draining is covered by the registration page.

Building work and carpentry:

- Whether landscaping work (paving, edging, wall work) counts as building work or a named trade in any state is UNCONFIRMED in this pass. Check the state building licence pages before lessons state a licence position.
- [NSW-01 to NSW-04] Contractor licence or supervisor certificate for specified trades above $5,000 (labour and materials, including GST). Landscaping is not a named trade in those rows.
- [QLD-01] QBCC building work over $3,300 needs a licence (general threshold). Application to landscaping work UNCONFIRMED.

Excavation:

- Before You Dig Australia is a free pre-excavation referral service (read; no date). Requests are made through an online portal with a free account; asset owners respond with plans.

Asbestos:

- [QLD-10, CONFIRMED] Class A and B licences. Class B for more than 10 m2 of non-friable asbestos.
- [VIC-32, CONFIRMED] Unlicensed non-friable removal up to 10 m2 and 1 hour in any seven days (OHS Regulations 2017).
- [NT-19, CONFIRMED] Friable any amount needs Class A; non-friable above 10 m2 needs a licence.
- [SA-19, CONFIRMED] Class A for friable and asbestos-backed material.

Biosecurity:

- Fire ant zone movement rules for soil, mulch and turf apply to landscaping materials (read; no date). Landscaping material itself is not named on the page; treat as UNCONFIRMED until checked.

## 6. Scope points for licence notices

- The lesson does not authorise retaining-wall design or construction, drainage connections, concrete works or structural work.
- Where a retaining wall or earth-retaining structure might need a permit, the lesson refers the client to the building authority.
- The lesson does not grant any building, plumbing or drainage licence.

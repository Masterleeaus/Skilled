# Research brief: Post-construction clean

- Course slug: `postcon`
- Group: Cleaning and hygiene
- Scope: Australia-wide (all states and territories). Victoria is not the default.
- Brief date: 2026-11-02
- Status: DRAFT. Reviewer status: NOT REVIEWED. Not verified, not published.
- Source key and UNCONFIRMED as defined in residential.md.

## 1. Scope and boundary

- Final clean of a finished or near-finished building after trades have left: fine dust removal, residue removal from glazing, fixtures, floors, and handover clean.
- Sits after the construction-site clean-up course. The construction course covers work on an active site. This course covers handover to a client.
- Out of scope: any removal of asbestos, lead, silica deposits, or unknown materials; any work on a site still under trade control; any certification of building compliance.

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- Handover clean method (top-down, dust control, glazing residue, fixtures, floors, sign-off): general practice. No Australian post-construction cleaning standard was confirmed. UNCONFIRMED.
- Dust control: the construction research (PRIOR) notes that visible cleanliness does not prove safety, and that wet methods and approved extraction are preferred over dry sweeping for silica-containing dust. Apply where the site assessment requires it.
- Engineered stone and benchtops: the national ban on manufacture, supply, processing and installation from 1 July 2024 (READ, SA Government). Installed engineered stone must not be cut, ground or altered in cleaning or touch-up work (READ, same page: work that removes, repairs or alters it should not be done).
- Surface damage and product selection (glazing residues, coatings, sealants): manufacturer instructions. UNCONFIRMED as a standard.
- Chemical selection and SDS: the Safe Work Australia SDS page is PRIOR only.
- Electrical equipment: AS/NZS 3760 (LISTED).
- Fall risk for high glazing and ceilings: see window brief and falls code (READ).

## 3. Main hazards

- Fine silica and dust from late-stage trades (grinding, plastering, tiling, cutting).
- Chemical exposure from glazing solvents, adhesives and concrete residue removers.
- Falls from step ladders and access equipment when cleaning high glazing or ceilings.
- Slips on newly installed floors and polished surfaces.
- Electrical hazards from wet methods near newly installed services (UNCONFIRMED standard for commissioning checks).
- Asbestos or lead in old fixtures or materials in refurbishment work (refer to asbestos code, READ for model status only).
- Manual handling of heavy glazing and fittings.

## 4. Regulator references

- Safe Work Australia model laws and model codes (READ on falls and asbestos pages; LISTED on plant and hazardous manual tasks codes).
- SafeWork NSW (READ), WorkSafe Victoria (READ), WorkSafe Queensland (LISTED), SafeWork SA (LISTED), WA and others: UNCONFIRMED.
- Building compliance and occupancy certification belong to state building regulators and certifiers. Not for cleaners. UNCONFIRMED per state. The Victorian Building Authority appears only in a listing for a gas standard (LISTED); the Victorian Building and Plumbing Commission appears in the prior register only (PRIOR).

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- Do not sign off building completion, compliance, or handover certificates. The cleaner records a clean condition and any defects only.
- Do not clean over unidentified residues. Stop and refer.
- No work on engineered stone beyond cleaning, unless a specialist has approved it.
- Course completion does not grant any licence or certification.

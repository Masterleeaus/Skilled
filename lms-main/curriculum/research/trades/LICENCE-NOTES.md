# Licence and authorisation notes: trade businesses (Victoria first)

Companion to LICENCE-MATRIX.csv in this folder. Checked 2026-11-02. Facts only, from the sources listed in the matrix. This is not legal advice.

## 1. Method and status key

- Sources: official government and regulator pages (BPC, VBA, Consumer Victoria, Victoria Police, WorkSafe Victoria, Energy Safe Victoria, asbestos.vic.gov.au, NSW Fair Trading and Building Commission NSW pages on nsw.gov.au, SafeWork NSW, NSW Police, Queensland Fair Trading, QBCC, WorkSafe Queensland, Electrical Safety Office Queensland pages).
- Only pages actually fetched are cited as evidence. Search-result links that were not opened are marked as such in the matrix.
- CONFIRMED: stated on an official page that was read.
- PARTIAL: part of the answer is on an official page; the rest is inferred or missing. The gap is noted in the row.
- UNCONFIRMED: no official source found in this pass, or the source is silent. Absence of a licence in this table means only "not found", not "does not exist".
- NOT RESEARCHED: out of scope for this pass.
- Dates: each row carries the date checked (2026-11-02). Source page dates are given in the notes where the page shows one. Several pages show no date or conflicting dates; these are flagged in section 6.

## 2. Victoria (VIC)

**Painting and plastering.** Consumer Victoria and BPC list painting and plastering as exempt trades for the domestic building registration rule when a job involves only one exempt trade. Registration is required once a domestic building job exceeds 10,000 dollars (contract value), or when a job combines skills, such as plastering and painting. The page does not say whether the 10,000 dollar cap also applies to combined jobs below that amount. That point is UNCONFIRMED. The 10,000 dollar threshold replaced 5,000 dollars from 1 August 2017 (VBA news item).

**Tiling including floating floors.** Wall and floor tiling is an exempt trade under the same rule. "Installing floor coverings" is also exempt. The source does not say whether a floating floor is a floor covering for this rule. UNCONFIRMED.

**Locksmith and door installation.** UNCONFIRMED. Victoria Police's private security pages (updated 2 November 2026) describe the Private Security Individual Operator Licence, the Private Security Business Licence, and the security equipment installer activity (CCTV, alarms, safes, vaults). Locksmithing and key cutting are not named. Do not state a Victorian locksmith licence exists or is absent until Licensing and Regulation Division confirms it. Door installation is not on the exemption list; whether it falls under carpentry or another trade is not stated.

**Handyman.** UNCONFIRMED as a category. The BPC/Consumer Victoria page does not mention handymen or carpenters. The general value rule and the combined-skills rule still apply to handyman jobs.

**Deck and fence.** Building permit rules are CONFIRMED from the VBA practice note BP-01 (dated April 2022 in the document). Attached decks need a permit at any size. Detached decks that form part of the amenity also need one. Fences up to 2 metres are exempt; street-side limits apply within 3 metres and 9 metres of intersections. Registration over 10,000 dollars is an inference, because decks and fences are not on the exemption list. Flagged PARTIAL.

**Pool and spa servicing.** No pool service licence was identified. Owners must keep barriers working (VBA). Barrier compliance certificates (Form 23 Certificate of pool and spa barrier compliance) are issued by registered building surveyors, building inspectors or pool inspectors (BPC, updated 30 June 2026). Barrier repairs are exempt from permit unless they cross the thresholds in BP-01 (for example more than 50 percent of barrier length).

**Pool chemical handling.** No licence was identified, but this is UNCONFIRMED. WorkSafe Victoria's alert of 22 April 2021 says chlorine and acid must be stored apart and dosed separately, with no dosing unless the filtration pump is running. A Safe Work Australia-style storage sheet gives segregation distances (3 m or barrier for up to 250 kg or L; 5 m for larger quantities). Its origin is not confirmed as a Safe Work Australia publication. The dangerous goods notification page does not mention pool chemicals, and its manifest quantities were not checked.

**Roof soft washing and solar panel cleaning.** No licence was identified for either. The WorkSafe falls compliance code is scoped to general construction; the page does not say whether roof cleaning is in scope. The ESV PV guideline does not mention cleaning. Both are UNCONFIRMED.

**Window and pressure cleaning at height.** A high risk work licence is needed to operate a boom-type elevating work platform (class WP; WorkSafe, reviewed 30 June 2026). WorkSafe states that scissor lifts do not need a high risk work licence. The licence class list does not include work at height, window cleaning or pressure cleaning. The only pressure-related classes are boilers, turbines and steam engines, not pressure washers. Whether general height duties apply to these tasks is UNCONFIRMED for cleaning work.

**Electrical isolation near solar systems.** The ESV/WorkSafe PV Installers Safety Guideline (stated as December 2018 in the document) says PV installation and maintenance is electrical installation work and must be done by licensed electrical installation workers. It advises isolation at the main switchboard with lock out and tag out, and notes that PV DC cables can remain live after the supply is off. The document refers to AS/NZS 4836. The licence class that covers PV isolation is UNCONFIRMED. ESV names four electrical licence types: A Grade licence, Registered electrical contractor's licence, Electrical inspector licence and Switchgear worker's licence. Its scope for PV is not stated. The ablis listing names Electrician's Licence (A Grade), supervised routes and electrical contractor registration.

**Gas and plumbing boundaries.** The BPC gasfitting class covers work on items downstream of the gas supply point, defined under Part 4 of the Plumbing Regulations 2018. It excludes cleaning, painting or preparing pipes without connecting or altering them. The licence route is registration, two years' experience, prescribed licence units and a BPC licence exam. ESV's gasfitter licences page directs enquiries to BPC. The BPC main classes include roofing stormwater plumbing (roof plumbing) and drainage plumbing. Who may connect gas appliances or work on the meter outlet is not stated. UNCONFIRMED.

**Asbestos (relevant to painting, plastering, tiling and demolition).** WorkSafe Victoria's licences are asbestos removal licence Class A (friable and non-friable) and Class B (non-friable only). Unlicensed removal of non-friable asbestos is allowed only up to 10 square metres in total and one hour in any seven days, with a matching limit for contaminated dust (asbestos.vic.gov.au; cites OHS Regulations 2017). The licence page shows a 5-year term and fees for 2026-27. The "10 square metres" limit was not on the licence page; it came from the unlicensed-removal page.

## 3. New South Wales (NSW) differences

- Painting, dry plastering, wall and floor tiling, and carpentry (including timber decks and timber and metal fencing) all need a contractor licence or qualified supervisor certificate for work over 5,000 dollars in labour and materials (including GST). Qualifications are listed per trade (for example CPC30620 for painting).
- Wet plastering, rendering and floating floors are not covered on the dry plastering and tiling pages. UNCONFIRMED.
- Plumbing, draining and gasfitting need a licence or certificate regardless of cost, with per-category licensing. Unlicensed work attracts fines up to 22,000 dollars (individual) or 110,000 dollars (company), per the page.
- Electrical wiring needs an electrical contractor licence or certificate regardless of cost. Installing solar panels requires a building or electrical contractor licence to contract, and an electrical licence holder for the wiring (page dated 19 October 2026). Solar cleaning and PV isolation are not covered: UNCONFIRMED.
- Locksmiths: individual locksmiths need a Class 2C licence under the NSW Police SLED scheme (FAQ, undated). Company or master licence requirements are UNCONFIRMED.
- Pools: owners must register on the Swimming Pool Register; certificates are needed for sale and lease; inspections are by councils or registered swimming pool inspectors. Pool technicians and fencing contractors have no specific licence stated. Minor barrier repairs under 1,000 dollars for a certificate need an endorsed contractor licence or qualified supervisor certificate for the certifier.
- Boom-type elevating work platform needs a WP high risk work licence (SafeWork NSW, dated 15 June 2026).
- Roof work: the new "Work on roofs - commercial and industrial buildings" code (May 2026, commenced 22 May 2026) covers cleaning on commercial and industrial roofs and excludes residential roofs. Soft washing and gutter cleaning were not addressed in the part read.
- Asbestos: SafeWork NSW lists Class A and Class B licences; thresholds were not stated on the page read.
- Deck and fence planning rules were not researched.

## 4. Queensland (QLD) differences

- QBCC licence applies to building work valued over 3,300 dollars (page updated 16 April 2026). Plumbing, drainage and gasfitting need a QBCC licence at any value.
- Painting and plastering class names were not found. Wall and floor tiling is listed as a QBCC class under "All other trade licences" (page last reviewed 31 August 2021). The tiling scope does not name floating floors or vinyl.
- Handyman work valued at 3,300 dollars or less, not electrical or plumbing and needing no occupational licence, does not need a QBCC licence (QBCC page dated 2021; check currency).
- Locksmiths need a security equipment installer licence under the Security Providers Act 1993 in most cases. Retail key cutting is exempt. Installing basic security items during construction, repair or renovation is exempt. Removing and reinstalling household locks needs a licence unless the building is under construction, repair or renovation (page updated 21 October 2026).
- Pool safety inspector (PSI) licence: inspect and certify; minor barrier repairs; erecting or replacing up to 5 metres and 6 posts. Maintenance and chemical servicing are not in scope (page date inconsistent).
- Electrical: work on energy generation and storage systems is electrical installation work needing an electrical mechanic licence on the ESO page; PV is not named. UNCONFIRMED for PV isolation and cleaning.
- Asbestos: Class A and Class B licences; no licence needed to remove 10 square metres or less of non-friable asbestos, but PCBU duties still apply. Homeowners removing over 10 square metres must hold a certificate.
- Boom-type elevating work platform: WP high risk work licence (platforms under 11 metres excluded by definition).

## 8. Implications for the Titan Academy licence rule

Under the DECISIONS.md licence rule, lessons covering licensed or authorised work need a short licence notice that says what the licence covers and that the lesson does not grant it. Suggested handling from this research:

- Electrical isolation near solar: notice should say PV maintenance electrical work is for licensed electrical workers; name only the licence classes the ESV page names; do not name which class covers isolation until confirmed.
- Window and pressure cleaning at height: notice should state the WP licence applies to boom-type platforms only, and that scissor lifts are outside it per WorkSafe Victoria.
- Locksmith: do not claim any Victorian licence until Licensing and Regulation Division confirms the position. Use state-specific notices for NSW (Class 2C) and QLD (security equipment installer) only where the lesson is state-specific.
- Pool chemicals: lessons may cite WorkSafe's separation and dosing advice, but must not imply a licence or a notification threshold until the dangerous goods quantities are checked.
- Asbestos: quote the 10 square metre and one hour limit for Victoria only with the source note.

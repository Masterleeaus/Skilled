# Regulation matrix notes: personal, pet and mobile services

Status: research draft for team review. Not legal advice. Every row in REGULATION-MATRIX.csv carries a source URL and a date checked. All pages were checked on 2026-11-02.

Companion file: REGULATION-MATRIX.csv (84 rows, 9 businesses, 3 jurisdictions, 6 topics).

## 1. Scope and method

- Businesses (from DECISIONS.md): mobile hairdressing and barbering (including colour and bleach); mobile beauty; mobile massage; personal and fitness training; yoga and Pilates; dog walking; pet and house sitting; mobile pet grooming; pet transport.
- Victoria first, then NSW and Queensland differences.
- Topics per business: licence or registration; council registration or permit; insurance; chemical or product rules; health screening or contraindications; extra rules for home or mobile work.
- Sources: state health and agriculture departments, WorkSafe Victoria, NSW Health, Queensland Health, and council pages. Industry bodies (AusActive) are marked SECONDARY. Business.gov.au (ablis) listings are used only as secondary copies of council or government text.
- Status key: CONFIRMED (official text read and quoted or paraphrased); COUNCIL-SPECIFIC (one council only, not state-wide); SECONDARY (industry or news source); UNCONFIRMED (not found, or wording too loose to rely on).
- Safe Work Australia's model code page was blocked by robots rules, so the Victorian WorkSafe pages stand in for it.

## 3. Victoria: findings by business

**Mobile hairdressing and barbering.** The Department of Health says hairdressing must register under the Public Health and Wellbeing Act 2008 (premises registration through the council). Macedon Ranges splits hairdressing into low-risk (ongoing registration) and high-risk (periodic renewal) services; that split is council-specific. For colour and bleach, WorkSafe lists PPD and peroxide in dyes, 7 to 12 per cent peroxide, powdered persulphate bleach and aerosol colours, with sensitisation and eye damage as the main hazards. The duties are the general ones: SDS, a hazardous substances register, GHS labelling, risk assessment, controls, PPE, training and consultation. The mobile question is open. The Department says mobile hairdressers "may" be exempt from registration except at their principal place of business; the Act and Regulations were not read.

**Mobile beauty.** Beauty therapy is listed as registrable under the same Act. Macedon Ranges puts facials, nails, electrolysis and waxing in the high-risk group, and tinting, spray tans and temporary make-up in the low-risk group. WorkSafe names nail products, eyelash glue and chemical peels. Injectable cosmetic treatments need a separate scope decision: the Department has EHO guidance on them (updated 22 May 2026), but the regime that applies to a mobile business was not confirmed.

**Mobile massage.** No Victorian state licence or premises registration for massage was found. The Department's registration notices and the Boroondara list do not include massage. A client information sheet exists (updated 2021), but its content was not read. Councils, product rules and insurance for massage are all UNCONFIRMED.

**Personal and fitness training.** No state licence was found in the sources checked. Councils are the active regulators: Stonnington requires a permit for personal training on public land, with public liability and professional indemnity policies, a Level 2 first aid certificate, registration with a peak body, participant caps and set hours. The AusActive pre-exercise screening tool is industry guidance (SECONDARY) and is not a statutory duty. If the business works with children, the Working with Children Check categories on the vic.gov.au page cover gyms and play facilities for children (code 32) and private coaching for children (code 28).

**Yoga and Pilates.** Same gaps as personal training. Stonnington's permit does not name yoga or Pilates, so council coverage is UNCONFIRMED. The children's-class categories apply only if the business fits them.

**Dog walking.** Dog walking is not among the Domestic Animal Business types listed by Agriculture Victoria, so no state registration was found. Councils regulate it on public land. Port Phillip requires a permit at four or more dogs, with limits of 8 per walker and 12 in total. Yarra requires a permit for any commercial dog exercising on council land (max 4 dogs, $153, expiry 10 April) and at least $20 million public liability naming the council. Port Phillip's fee dates conflict within its own page, so that must be confirmed with the council.

**Pet and house sitting.** The Domestic Animals Act treats boarding as caring for a dog or cat for fee or reward at a property other than its normal place of residence. Every boarding establishment must register with the council. Agriculture Victoria describes home boarding (limited to two animals from one household) as a form of boarding. Stonnington lists for-profit homecare boarding as needing a permit. On that wording, house sitting in the owner's home does not obviously fall within "boarding", but this is an interpretation and is marked UNCONFIRMED. The Boarding Code (mandatory for registered boarding businesses) requires $10 million public liability, vaccination certificates before admission, on-call vet arrangements, records and trained staff. Whether any of this applies to sitters is UNCONFIRMED.

**Mobile pet grooming.** Grooming is not among the listed Domestic Animal Business types (Agriculture Victoria or Stonnington). No council registration for mobile groomers was confirmed. Product and animal-welfare rules were not established.

**Pet transport.** No Victorian pet-taxi licence or permit was found. The Boarding Code says the driver is responsible for animal welfare in the vehicle, but that applies to boarding establishments. The livestock transport code is written for livestock and its application to pets is UNCONFIRMED.

**Insurance (all businesses).** WorkCover registration is generally required when employing any workers. A sole trader with no other workers may not need it. A contractor may be a deemed worker of the business it depends on. Public liability is not a general state requirement for these trades as far as found. Councils and the Boarding Code set the amounts that appear in practice ($10m, $20m, or "current and naming the council").

**Home or mobile work (all businesses).** WorkSafe says self-employed people must ensure their work does not expose others to risk, so far as reasonably practicable, and that employers must do the same for customers and the public. The definition of "workplace" covers any place where self-employed people work. WorkSafe does not say directly whether a client's home counts. Treat this as an interpretation for confirmation.

**Chemicals (all hair and beauty and general).** From 1 December 2026, Safe Work Australia replaces the Workplace Exposure Standards list with the Workplace Exposure Limits list (per WorkSafe, reviewed 19 May 2026). This date is within the next month and affects any exposure reference the team cites.

## 4. NSW and Queensland differences

- **Hairdressing (NSW):** not regulated under the skin penetration rules unless it deliberately pierces the skin; councils may regulate under other laws. Mobile operators working from their own vehicle or a temporary structure must notify council. In-home notification is not stated.
- **Hairdressing and beauty (QLD):** not higher-risk personal appearance services, so no licence. Higher-risk services (cosmetic tattooing, semi-permanent make-up, implanting substances, piercing) need a higher-risk licence and HLTINF005 competency. Who issues the licence is not stated on the Qld Health page.
- **Dog walking (NSW):** councils run permit systems on public land. Ryde requires a commercial minder permit, $10 million APRA public liability, up to 4 dogs, and a yearly fee of $367 to $1,470 depending on location and hours. Inner West's proposed fee-and-permit scheme was deferred in 2023.
- **Dog walking (QLD):** the Brisbane page does not mention commercial dog walking permits. Not confirmed elsewhere.
- **Massage, personal training, yoga, grooming, transport:** not checked for NSW or Queensland in this pass.

## 6. Decisions taken for review

- Treated "Dept of Health says may" wording as UNCONFIRMED rather than an exemption.
- Treated the Boarding Code (Revision 1, 2004, printed 2025) as current for the purposes of this matrix, since the Agriculture Victoria page points to it as the mandatory code. The "2025" in the filename is a print date, not a revision date.
- Treated ablis.business.gov.au listings as secondary copies of government text, not primary sources. Official primary pages were used wherever they were allowed.
- Treated Stonnington's permit as an example of council practice, not a state requirement.
- Did not treat the AusActive tool as a legal requirement.
- Used the WorkSafe Victoria pages in place of the Safe Work Australia model code, which was blocked.
- Listed injectables under mobile beauty as "scope decision" rather than adding them to the business list.

## 7. Caveats

- Council rules differ widely. Only Port Phillip, Yarra, Stonnington, Macedon Ranges, Boroondara and Ryde (NSW) were read. Other councils are not represented.
- Official pages change. The dates in section 2 are when this research read them, not when the law took effect.
- Nothing here is legal advice. Items marked UNCONFIRMED need a check against the Act, Regulations or the regulator before anyone relies on them.

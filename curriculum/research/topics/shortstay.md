## 1. Scope and boundary

- Turnover cleaning between guest stays in short-term rental accommodation: check-out, linen and amenity restock, inspection and reset.
- Shared lessons: kitchen and bathroom (DECISIONS.md).
- Out of scope: hosting or property management as a business, pricing, platform management, and any statement that the cleaner is the registered host.
- Out of scope: biohazard, mould, pest infestation, fire and smoke, and damage restoration (High-risk module or other courses).

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- Turnover method (checklists, linen handling, sanitising high-touch surfaces, inventory): general practice. No Australian turnover standard was confirmed. UNCONFIRMED.
- Regulation of short-stay accommodation is set by state law and sometimes by local councils and owners corporations. Current NSW position (READ):
  - The NSW Code of conduct for the short-term rental accommodation industry sets obligations for hosts and for property managers and facilitators.
  - Host obligations include insurance for third-party injury and death, contactability, emergency contact information, providing the premises in the condition advertised, and notifying the owners corporation and neighbours.
  - Property managers must advertise or facilitate stays only for registered hosts and premises, once registration applies.
  - The code sets no specific cleaning standard, no cleaning schedule, and does not mention cleaners by name. Whether a cleaner is a "facilitator" under the code is not stated: UNCONFIRMED.
- Victoria: owners corporation rules can restrict short stays (LISTED, Consumer Affairs Victoria page on making rules to ban short-stay accommodation). Current Victorian state rules for short stays: UNCONFIRMED.
- Queensland: approvals for accommodation appear in council-level listings (LISTED). State-level position: UNCONFIRMED.
- Other states and territories: UNCONFIRMED.
- Electrical equipment: AS/NZS 3760 (LISTED). Gas appliances: state gasfitting rules and AS/NZS 5601.1 (LISTED) apply to any gas appliance work, which is out of scope.

## 3. Main hazards

- Slips in wet bathrooms and kitchens on turnover.
- Chemical exposure from frequent use of sanitisers and degreasers in small rooms. Ventilation and SDS required.
- Falls from step ladders when changing light fittings or high cupboards.
- Manual handling of linen, bins and furniture.
- Electrical faults in guest appliances (AS/NZS 3760, LISTED).
- Unrecognised problems that guests can create (damage, pests, biological or drug contamination). Stop and report. Do not clean over them. Drug contamination and biohazard are in the High-risk module.
- Lone working in a property the cleaner does not control: UNCONFIRMED state guidance.

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- No licence or registration is granted by the course. Host and premises registration is the host's duty, not the cleaner's.
- Do not hold keys, advertise, set rates or represent the property as compliant.
- Do not inspect or certify smoke alarms, pools, balconies or safety features. Report defects.
- Stop and refer any hazard that requires specialist work.

## 1. Scope and boundary

- Offices, retail, shared and public-facing commercial premises.
- Per DECISIONS.md, this course also holds these lessons: warehouse cleaning, laboratory cleaning, restaurants and commercial kitchens (including commercial kitchen exhaust), food-processing sanitation, and industrial degreasing. Bathroom and kitchen lessons are shared with residential.
- Out of scope unless the High-risk cleaning module applies: biohazard, mould, fire and smoke, flood and crime-scene work.
- Laboratory decontamination, biological waste and biosafety-level work are specialist programmes, not housekeeping (see prior laboratory research). Do not teach them as general cleaning.

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- Routine office and amenities cleaning: general practice. Australian method standard: UNCONFIRMED.
- Hazardous manual tasks (lifting, pushing, prolonged reaching): Safe Work Australia Model Code of Practice: Hazardous manual tasks. LISTED. State adoption: UNCONFIRMED.
- Plant (floor scrubbers, extractors, ride-on machines, cleaning carts and access equipment): Safe Work Australia Model Code of Practice: Managing risks of plant in the workplace. LISTED. Two state versions also appear in search results (NSW code 2022; NT and Tasmania versions), LISTED.
- Chemicals: SDS and labelling duties. The Safe Work Australia SDS page is PRIOR only.
- Food premises (kitchens, food-processing sanitation): Food Standards Code, Standard 3.2.2 (food safety practices and general requirements). PRIOR. Text not read this pass. Its application to contract cleaners in each premises: UNCONFIRMED.
- Commercial kitchen exhaust fire systems: AS 1851-2012 (routine service of fire protection systems and equipment). LISTED (store listing). AS/NZS 1668.1 is a related installation standard, LISTED (1998 edition in a listing). Current edition and the relationship to kitchen-exhaust cleaning: UNCONFIRMED.
- Forklifts, racking and loading docks in warehouse cleaning: WorkSafe Victoria pages on forklift hazards and pallet racking are PRIOR only.
- Laboratory work: WHO Laboratory Biosafety Manual (fourth edition) is PRIOR only. Not read.

## 3. Main hazards

- Plant and moving equipment (floor machines, carts, vehicles and forklifts in shared areas).
- Manual handling of bins, stock, furniture and machines.
- Slips on wet floors and in food areas.
- Chemical exposure (concentrates, degreasers, oven and grease cleaners). SDS and ventilation required.
- Falls from ladders and working at height (window, ceiling and high-bay work). See window brief for height rules.
- Electrical equipment (AS/NZS 3760, LISTED).
- Fire risk from grease in kitchen exhausts. The prior kitchen-exhaust research states grease deposits can become an ignition hazard (PRIOR). The NSW Fire and Rescue restaurant safety page is PRIOR.
- Biological and sharps hazards in amenities: UNCONFIRMED per state.

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- A cleaning worker does not isolate or repair plant, change racking, operate forklifts or handle unknown chemicals or spills.
- Kitchen exhaust cleaning to a fire-safety standard is specialist work. This course does not certify it. Certification claims are not allowed.
- Laboratory and biological decontamination is not general cleaning.
- No licence or authority is granted by completing the course.

- Matrix rows used: MBEAU-*, NAT-MBEAU-*, HAIR-VIC-*, NAT-ALL-*, NAT-CHEM-*, ALL-VIC-02, CHEM-VIC-01 in curriculum/research/personal/REGULATION-MATRIX-NATIONAL.csv.

## 1. Business scope

- One business: mobile beauty, from DECISIONS.md.
- Decision on record: cosmetic injectables (botulinum toxin, dermal filler) are EXCLUDED from this business. This brief records the legal evidence for that decision. It does not reopen it.
- Hair colour and barbering belong to the hair course.
- Services named in the matrix as beauty work include facials, nail services, electrolysis, waxing, tinting, spray tans, temporary make-up and eyelash work. The Hair and Beauty Industry Award also names waxing and nail services (MA000005).

## 2. Regulatory position

**Licence or registration**
- VIC: CONFIRMED. Beauty therapy is a registrable service under the Public Health and Wellbeing Act 2008. Registration is of premises, handled through the council (MBEAU-VIC-01).
- VIC, council level: COUNCIL-SPECIFIC. Macedon Ranges puts facials, nail services, electrolysis and waxing in the high-risk group, which needs renewal and a fee each time. Tinting, spray tans, temporary make-up and henna sit in the low-risk group (MBEAU-VIC-02). This is one council only.
- NSW: UNCONFIRMED. NSW Health lists beauty treatments as regulated, but does not state a separate notification rule for beauty-only work. The page refers to premises where skin penetration happens (MBEAU-NSW-01).
- QLD: CONFIRMED. Beauty therapy is not a higher-risk service, so no licence is needed. Higher-risk services (cosmetic tattooing, semi-permanent make-up, implanting substances under the skin, piercing, scarring) need a higher-risk licence (MBEAU-QLD-01; NAT-MBEAU-QLD-01 from Gold Coast).
- QLD competency conflict: UNCONFIRMED. The earlier row cites a competency unit (HLTINF005) for higher-risk operators. Townsville's sheet names no competency, and the Gold Coast page does not name it either. The source must be found before the row is used (NAT-MBEAU-QLD-04).
- WA: UNCONFIRMED. The WA hairdressing guideline covers hairdressing only. Beauty and skin penetration rules were not read (NAT-MBEAU-WA-01).
- SA: COUNCIL-SPECIFIC. Port Augusta covers hairdressing, beauty and skin penetration under one notification form. The state rule for beauty-only work is UNCONFIRMED (NAT-MBEAU-SA-01).
- TAS: UNCONFIRMED. Beauty is not named in the reviewed public health risk list. Hobart's registration page says approval covers premises only and that activities need separate licensing. Council to confirm (NAT-MBEAU-TAS-01).
- ACT: UNCONFIRMED. Skin penetration businesses must hold an infection control licence. It is unclear whether beauty work without skin penetration needs one (NAT-MBEAU-ACT-01).
- NT: CONFIRMED. Registration covers beauty therapy (face and body treatments, manicures and pedicures, artificial nails, semi-permanent make-up and cosmetic tattooing) and body art. Registrations last one year (NAT-MBEAU-NT-01). Fee on the NT page: 141 dollars where beauty or body art is offered, alone or with hairdressing.

**Mobile and home visits**
- VIC: UNCONFIRMED. The Department of Health says mobile beauty therapists and mobile cosmetic application services (without skin penetration or tattooing) "may" be exempt from registration except at their principal place of business. The Act and Regulations were not read (MBEAU-VIC-06).
- NSW: CONFIRMED for vehicle or temporary-structure operators, who must notify council. Other venues and homes are referred to the Regulation without a stated notification rule (MBEAU-NSW-02).
- ACT: CONFIRMED for mobile skin penetration services, which fall under the licence. Home work UNCONFIRMED (NAT-MBEAU-ACT-02).
- QLD: UNCONFIRMED for beauty-only work. Townsville says mobile higher-risk operations must be licensed. Beauty-only work is not higher-risk, so that rule does not obviously reach it, but the home-visit position was not confirmed (NAT-HAIR-QLD-02, Townsville).
- WA, SA, TAS, NT: UNCONFIRMED for mobile or home work.

**Insurance (workers compensation)**
- The same employer rules as the hair course apply. See hair.md section 2 for NSW, QLD, WA, SA, TAS, ACT and NT (NAT-ALL-*). VIC: HAIR-VIC-03.
- Public liability: UNCONFIRMED at state level (MBEAU-VIC-04 and the hair course's HAIR-VIC-04 apply to this business in the same way).

**Chemicals and products**
- VIC general duties: CONFIRMED. The same SDS, register, GHS label, risk assessment, control and training duties apply as for hairdressing (HAIR-VIC-05).
- VIC product hazards: CONFIRMED. WorkSafe names nail acrylics and dip powders (mists and dust), eyelash extension glue (a cyanoacrylate, with risk of sensitisation and contact dermatitis), and chemical peels (irritation and eye damage) (MBEAU-VIC-04).
- National model WHS: CONFIRMED as a model position (NAT-CHEM-01). State adoption to confirm.
- Exposure limits: CONFIRMED (WorkSafe Victoria). The Workplace Exposure Limits list replaces the Workplace Exposure Standards list from 1 December 2026 (ALL-VIC-02).

**Health screening and contraindications**
- VIC: UNCONFIRMED. The Department of Health says beauty procedures can transmit infection and that the 2019 infection prevention guidelines apply. Screening, contraindication and patch-test content was not read (MBEAU-VIC-05).
- Other states: UNCONFIRMED. No screening rule was read for beauty in NSW, QLD, WA, SA, TAS, ACT or NT.

**Injectable cosmetic treatments (scope decision recorded)**
- QLD: CONFIRMED that Queensland Health says cosmetic injectable businesses must follow medicines laws, including businesses using services based interstate. The page names a registered nurse as an example of an authorised administrator and does not mention beauty therapists (NAT-MBEAU-QLD-03).
- QLD council: COUNCIL-SPECIFIC. Gold Coast treats cosmetic injectables as implanting substances under the skin, which is licensable. Townsville does not name them (NAT-MBEAU-QLD-02).
- TAS: CONFIRMED. The Department of Health lists botulinum toxin and deoxycholic acid as Schedule 4 medicines. Registered nurses need written authorisation. Non-registered people may not obtain, possess or administer them for cosmetic use unless lawfully prescribed for their own treatment (NAT-MBEAU-TAS-02). Mobile supervision is UNCONFIRMED (NAT-MBEAU-TAS-03).
- VIC: UNCONFIRMED. EHO guidance on injectables exists (dated 22 May 2026) but the regime for a mobile business is not confirmed (MBEAU-VIC-03).
- NT, ACT, WA, SA: UNCONFIRMED.
- Decision on record: excluded from mobile beauty (DECISIONS.md). If the decision changes, every lesson on the topic needs a licence notice and a medicines check.

## 5. Scope boundaries and licence notices

- Injectables, piercing, tattooing and implanting substances are out of scope for this business (DECISIONS.md and section 2 above).
- Semi-permanent make-up and cosmetic tattooing are higher-risk services in Queensland and are in the NT registration list. Keep them out of this course unless the scope changes.
- Waxing and eyelash tinting are named beauty work. Their state rules vary, so each lesson must say which states it has been checked for.
- A lesson on hygiene must not imply that finishing the course authorises beauty work in any state.
- If a lesson covers a licensed or registered service (for example, Queensland higher-risk work), it needs a licence notice that says what the licence covers and that the lesson does not grant it.

## 1. Scope and boundaries

In scope:
- Assembly of flat-pack furniture using the manufacturer's instructions and parts check.
- Wall fixing of non-electrical fixtures: shelves, mirrors, curtain rails, towel rails, and non-electrical TV mounts, within the manufacturer's load rating.
- Anchoring of furniture to walls to prevent tip-over, following the supplier's anchoring information.
- Replacement of simple hardware (handles, hinges, drawer runners).

Out of scope or referred:
- Any electrical connection, including light fittings, power points, switches, TV power, and hard-wired smoke alarms (licensed electrician; see section 5).
- Plumbing or gas connections (appliance or sink connection, licensed plumber or gasfitter).
- Heavy or engineered fixtures (for example, large mirrors, heavy cabinets, or wall-hung items that need a structural fixing plan). Refer to an engineer or specialist.
- Structural changes to walls, lintels, or load-bearing elements.
- Asbestos or lead in old wall linings (see section 5).

## 2. Key technical methods

- Parts check: match parts against the manufacturer's list before starting; keep fixings sorted; set the assembly area clear.
- Instruction reading: follow the manufacturer's sequence; do not fit parts out of order where the instructions forbid it.
- Wall type identification: plasterboard (stud or cavity), brick, block, concrete, or hollow core. Method: tap test, stud finder, and a small test hole where the wall is not known. Locating services before drilling is a technique point (no national method source checked).
- Fixing choice: use the fixing type rated for the wall and load; plasterboard fixings and stud fixings differ. Ratings from the manufacturer's data sheet; no national standard for each fixing identified in this pass (UNCONFIRMED).
- Anchoring: fix the anchor point into a stud, or into a masonry anchor where the wall requires it; use the supplier's anchor kit. Record the fixing type and location.
- Levels and alignment: check with a level, and adjust before final tightening.
- Records: parts used, fixing type, wall type, photos of anchoring, and client sign-off.

## 4. Main hazards

- Tip-over of furniture and wall-mounted items onto people; children at highest risk (per the ACCC page: at least 28 deaths since 2000 from toppling furniture and televisions, 17 of them children under five; read).
- Electric shock from drilling into cables or from electrical fixture work (licensed work).
- Water damage from drilling into pipes.
- Falls from ladders and step-ups.
- Manual handling of flat-pack boxes, panels and heavy furniture.
- Pinch points and sharp edges on hardware and panels.
- Dust from drilling masonry or old plaster (silica exposure limit not checked in this pass).
- Asbestos or lead in old wall linings, sealants or paint (matrix thresholds in section 5).

- [NSW-07, CONFIRMED] Electrical wiring work, including repair, needs an electrical contractor licence holder or certificate regardless of cost. The page does not name fixture installation specifically.
- [QLD-07, PARTIAL] Electrical installation work on energy systems; general fixture work not named.
- [VIC-33, PARTIAL] Electrician's licences listed; scope per licence not stated.
- [WA-21, PARTIAL] Electrical installing work includes PV and battery equipment; general fixtures not named.
- [SA-15, PARTIAL] Electrical licence and registration needed for electrical installation work (classes not read).
- [TAS-14, PARTIAL] Electrical licence types named; scope not stated.
- [ACT-15, PARTIAL] Electrician licence classes named; scope not stated.
- [NT-14, PARTIAL] Unrestricted electrical mechanic licence for electrical installation and maintenance; PV not named; fixture work status not stated in the row read.

Plumbing and gas:
- [NSW-06, CONFIRMED] Plumbing and gasfitting licence at any cost. Connecting or altering pipes is licensed work.
- [QLD-06, CONFIRMED] QBCC licence for plumbing and gasfitting at any value.

Handyman boundary:
- [QLD-05, PARTIAL] Handyman work valued at $3,300 or less, not electrical or plumbing, needs no occupational licence. Page dated 2021; check currency.
- [NSW-05, UNCONFIRMED] Minor trade work page is inconsistent (see `handyman.md` section 5).

Asbestos (older walls):
- [QLD-10, CONFIRMED], [VIC-32, CONFIRMED], [WA-26, CONFIRMED], [SA-20, CONFIRMED], [TAS-19, CONFIRMED], [NT-19, CONFIRMED]: thresholds as in `handyman.md` section 5.

Product safety:
- ACCC toppling furniture information standard (section 3). The standard places duties on suppliers, not on installers. Lessons should describe anchoring as good practice and as the supplier's instruction.

## 6. Scope points for licence notices

- The lesson does not authorise any electrical connection or plumbing or gas work. Such work is referred to a licensed trade.
- Where a fixing is for a heavy or engineered item, the lesson refers the client.
- The lesson does not grant any licence or permit.

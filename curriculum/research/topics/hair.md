# Mobile hairdressing and barbering: research brief

- Course slug: `hair` (from curriculum/structure/COURSES-MODULES.json)
- Status: DRAFT research brief. Reviewer status: NOT REVIEWED. Not legal advice.
- Scope: Australia-wide. Victoria is not the default.
- Dates: all sources below were checked on 2026-11-02. Items marked "located only" were found by search and were not read.
- Matrix rows used: HAIR-VIC-*, HAIR-NSW-*, HAIR-QLD-*, NAT-HAIR-*, NAT-ALL-*, NAT-CHEM-*, ALL-VIC-02, CHEM-VIC-01 in curriculum/research/personal/REGULATION-MATRIX-NATIONAL.csv.

## 1. Business scope

- One business, from DECISIONS.md: mobile hairdressing and barbering.
- Matrix scope includes colour and bleach work.
- Waxing, nail work and beauty treatments belong to the mobile beauty course, not this one. The Hair and Beauty Industry Award names waxing and nail services as covered work (see section 7), so a team decision is needed if this business offers them.
- Injectables, piercing, tattooing and skin penetration are outside this business.

## 2. Regulatory position

Status labels are kept from the matrix.

**Licence or registration**
- VIC: CONFIRMED. Hairdressing is a registrable service under the Public Health and Wellbeing Act 2008. Registration is of premises, handled by the council. No individual practitioner licence is described (HAIR-VIC-01).
- VIC, council level: COUNCIL-SPECIFIC. Macedon Ranges separates low-risk and high-risk hairdressing with different renewal rules. Boroondara lists hairdressing as registrable. Other councils are unconfirmed (HAIR-VIC-02).
- NSW: CONFIRMED. NSW Health says hairdressing is not regulated under the skin penetration rules unless the service deliberately pierces the skin. Councils may regulate it under other laws (HAIR-NSW-01).
- NSW, council level: COUNCIL-SPECIFIC. Ryde's page says hairdressing premises must register, but the body text was not retrieved, so treat this as a lead (NAT-HAIR-NSW-01).
- QLD: CONFIRMED. Hairdressing is not a higher-risk personal appearance service, so no licence is needed. The Public Health (Infection Control for Personal Appearance Services) Act 2003, the Regulation and the 2024 Infection Control Guidelines still apply (HAIR-QLD-01; Gold Coast reading in NAT-HAIR-QLD-01).
- WA: CONFIRMED. The Chief Health Officer guideline for hairdressing is issued under the Public Health Act 2016. The retrieved text describes no state licence (NAT-HAIR-WA-01).
- SA: COUNCIL-SPECIFIC. SA Health describes a general duty under the Public Health Act 2011 for hairdressing salons. Port Augusta asks for a notification form. No state registration is described (NAT-HAIR-SA-01).
- TAS: UNCONFIRMED. Hairdressing is not named in the reviewed public health risk list. Hobart registers premises for risk activities and advises checking scope (NAT-HAIR-TAS-01).
- ACT: CONFIRMED. No infection control licence for hairdressing. The Infection Control Code of Practice 2005 applies (NAT-HAIR-ACT-01).
- NT: CONFIRMED. A business that receives payment for hairdressing must register with NT Government. The page gives a fee and does not name the Act (NAT-HAIR-NT-01).

**Mobile and home visits**
- VIC: UNCONFIRMED. The Department of Health says mobile hairdressers "may" be exempt from registration except at their principal place of business. The Act and Regulations were not read (HAIR-VIC-09). Treat this as an open item, not an exemption.
- NSW: CONFIRMED for vehicle or temporary-structure operators, who must notify council. Notification for in-home work is UNCONFIRMED (HAIR-NSW-02).
- QLD: UNCONFIRMED. The Qld Health page does not address mobile or home work (HAIR-QLD-02). Gold Coast says mobile and temporary premises can apply for higher-risk licences, but hairdressing is not higher-risk (NAT-HAIR-QLD-02).
- WA: CONFIRMED. Guideline section 13.1 covers mobile operators. Mobile operators need hand-washing facilities with warm water, soap and paper towels, a treatment area, cleaning of equipment between clients, a sharps container, and wastewater to sewer, not stormwater (NAT-HAIR-WA-02). Home-based operators must meet the same requirements and contact local government before starting (NAT-HAIR-WA-03).
- ACT: UNCONFIRMED for home work. The ACT page says some business types may be restricted to premises (NAT-HAIR-ACT-02).
- SA, TAS, NT: UNCONFIRMED.
- National: UNCONFIRMED whether a client's home is a workplace for WHS purposes. Safe Work Australia's working-from-home page covers workers at home, not client homes (NAT-CHEM-02). WorkSafe Victoria's duty-to-others wording may apply (HAIR-VIC-10).

**Insurance (workers compensation)**
- VIC: CONFIRMED. WorkCover registration is generally required when employing any worker (HAIR-VIC-03).
- NSW: CONFIRMED. Employers with workers generally need a policy. Employers paying 7,500 dollars or less in annual wages with no apprentices or trainees are exempt from holding a policy but must still report injuries. Sole traders with no workers do not need a policy (NAT-ALL-NSW-01).
- QLD: CONFIRMED for employers. A contractor under a contract of service is a worker even with an ABN. The page does not address sole traders with no workers (NAT-ALL-QLD-01).
- WA: CONFIRMED for employers. Sole traders and contractors not addressed (NAT-ALL-WA-01).
- SA: CONFIRMED. Register within 14 days of employing workers. The page states a wages threshold for exemption (NAT-ALL-SA-01).
- TAS: CONFIRMED for employers, with a citation to check (NAT-ALL-TAS-01).
- ACT: CONFIRMED. Engaging a worker under a contract creates an employer relationship; a regular, systematic contractor can be deemed a worker (NAT-ALL-ACT-01).
- NT: CONFIRMED. Employers with a worker must arrange a policy (NAT-ALL-NT-01).
- Public liability: UNCONFIRMED at state level (HAIR-VIC-04).

**Chemicals and product**
- VIC general duties: CONFIRMED. Obtain a current SDS for each hazardous substance and keep it accessible. Keep a hazardous substances register. Use GHS labels. Do a risk assessment. Apply the hierarchy of controls. Provide PPE, information, training and supervision. Consult workers. Label decanted substances with the product identifier, with limited exceptions (HAIR-VIC-05).
- VIC colour and bleach hazards: CONFIRMED. WorkSafe names PPD and hydrogen peroxide in dyes, peroxide solutions at 7 to 12 per cent, powdered persulphate bleach, and aerosol colours (HAIR-VIC-06). Suggested controls on that page (PPD-free products, dust-free bleach, a separate mixing area) are suggestions, not stated duties.
- National model WHS: CONFIRMED as a model position. A PCBU keeps a register of hazardous chemicals with current SDS. State adoption must be confirmed (NAT-CHEM-01).
- WA product rules: CONFIRMED. Dye mixing bowls cleaned after each use. No food preparation where dyes are mixed. Blood-contaminated equipment cleaned, then immersed in 70 per cent alcohol or a hospital-grade disinfectant. Products labelled with name, date and dilution (NAT-HAIR-WA-04).
- Exposure limits: CONFIRMED (WorkSafe Victoria, reviewed 19 May 2026). From 1 December 2026 the Workplace Exposure Standards list is replaced by the Workplace Exposure Limits list. Any exposure reference in a lesson should use the list in force on the day (ALL-VIC-02).
- Health monitoring: UNCONFIRMED for hairdressers. WorkSafe's hairdressing page does not set out monitoring duties (HAIR-VIC-07). The general page says employers may need air monitoring and health surveillance depending on exposure (CHEM-VIC-01).

**Health screening and infection control**
- VIC: UNCONFIRMED. The 2019 infection prevention guidelines apply but were not read (HAIR-VIC-08).
- WA: CONFIRMED. No formal screening of staff or clients. For clients with an open wound, infection or skin condition, the guideline advises a GP visit first. Head lice do not stop a client attending (NAT-HAIR-WA-05).
- SA: UNCONFIRMED. A guideline is referenced but was not read (NAT-HAIR-SA-03).
- NT: UNCONFIRMED. The registration page names no specific infection control duties (NAT-HAIR-NT-03).

## 5. Scope boundaries and licence notices

- A lesson that covers council or state registration must say that the business, not the course, is what is registered. Completing the course does not register the business.
- Lessons on colour and bleach may cite the SDS and the regulator pages above. They must not give product concentrations beyond those in the sources.
- Shaving and beard work sit close to skin penetration. Whether they count as skin penetration in any state is UNCONFIRMED. Do not describe blade work on the skin as outside scope until checked.
- Waxing, nail work, eyelash work and tinting are beauty-scope tasks. Keep them out of this course unless the team decides otherwise.
- No licence for hairdressing practitioners was found in the states read. Do not imply that any licence exists.

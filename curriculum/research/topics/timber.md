## 1. Scope and boundary

- Restoration of timber floors: sanding, screening, re-coating, staining, sealing, and board repair.
- Out of scope: structural timber repair, subfloor work, installation of new floors (flooring trade), and work in buildings with suspected lead or asbestos without a clearance or specialist plan.
- Hard-floor maintenance is in the hard floor course.

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- Sanding, screening and coating method: manufacturer instructions and industry practice. No Australian method standard confirmed. UNCONFIRMED.
- Wood dust (WorkSafe Victoria, READ, reviewed 17 April 2026):
  - Health effects include respiratory effects, skin conditions and nasal cancer. The page cites the International Agency for Research on Cancer for the carcinogenic classification of wood dust.
  - Controls follow the hierarchy: use pre-cut or processed timber where possible, use local exhaust ventilation at each source, use on-tool extraction connected to a vacuum rated at least Dust Class M, and use wet cleaning and HEPA vacuums (minimum Dust Class M). The page recommends damping down before sweeping and does not expressly prohibit dry sweeping.
  - Do not use compressed air to clear dust from benches, skin or clothing.
  - Respiratory protection is a last resort. A half-face respirator with a P2 filter is usually sufficient; equipment should meet AS/NZS 1716; facial-seal respirators need clean-shaven users and fit testing.
  - Exposure standards per the page: softwood dust 5 mg/m3 (8-hour TWA) and 10 mg/m3 (STEL); certain hardwoods such as beech and oak 1 mg/m3 TWA; formaldehyde from MDF and particleboard 1 ppm TWA and 2 ppm STEL.
  - Check these values against the current national exposure standard list before teaching them. A December 2026 change to the exposure-limit scheme is noted in the prior register (PRIOR; not verified). UNCONFIRMED for current values.
- Solvent and stain products: SDS duties. The Safe Work Australia SDS page is PRIOR only.
- Electrical equipment (sanders, polishers): AS/NZS 3760 (LISTED).
- Lead in old floor finishes: see furniture brief (NSW LeadSmart, LISTED).

## 3. Main hazards

- Wood dust from sanding and screening (respiratory, skin and cancer risk). Dust Class M extraction and respirator controls per page.
- Formaldehyde from engineered boards.
- Fine dust explosion risk: UNCONFIRMED guidance this pass.
- Solvent and stain vapours in enclosed rooms.
- Electrical risk from wet floors and extension leads.
- Noise from sanders: UNCONFIRMED state guidance.
- Hazardous materials in old finishes (lead, asbestos adhesives). Stop and refer if suspected.
- Slips on sanding residue and wet finishes.

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- Do not sand or screen a floor without a dust-control plan, extraction and approved respiratory protection where the plan requires it.
- Do not dry-sweep wood dust. Use damping and HEPA extraction.
- Do not use compressed air to clear dust.
- Do not declare a floor structurally sound or free from hazard.
- Completing the course does not grant any licence.

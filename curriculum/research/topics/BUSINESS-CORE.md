# Shared business lessons: research brief (BUSINESS-CORE)

- Status: DRAFT research brief. Reviewer status: NOT REVIEWED. Not legal or tax advice.
- Scope: Australia-wide. Federal sources (ATO, Fair Work, ACCC, ACMA, OAIC, business.gov.au) apply everywhere. State and council rules are cross-referenced to the regulation matrices in curriculum/research/personal/ and to the matching trades files.
- Dates: sources were checked on 2026-11-02. "Read" means the page was fetched. "Located only" means it appeared in search and was not read. "Read via matrix" means the content came from the regulation matrix, not from a new fetch.
- Figures: this brief gives no industry rates, prices, margins or thresholds of its own. Where a figure appears, it is copied from a cited page and dated. Figures that a page states may change; re-check before publishing.
- Source warning: the Fair Work small business pay page prints a national minimum wage figure dated from 1 July 2023. Do not use it. The current figure must be taken from the Fair Work Ombudsman or Fair Work Commission pages for the year in force.

## 1. Starting a home-service business

**What the lesson should cover:** the sequence from ABN to registrations, licences and insurance, with state and council differences marked.

- ABN: the ATO lists approved ABN forms by entity type, including sole traders (read, 25 Mar 2026). Who must hold an ABN is not stated on that page. UNCONFIRMED: the general test. Locate the ATO "who needs an ABN" page before publishing.
- Structure: the ATO business structures page (located only) sets out key tax obligations by structure. Not read. Choice of structure is UNCONFIRMED in this brief.
- Registrations and licences: the matrix rows are state- and council-specific (see the eight personal, fitness and pet briefs and the trades matrices). Keep each lesson's statement tied to a state or council. Do not state one national licence.
- Workers compensation: employer rules differ by state (CONFIRMED for the states read; see personal/REGULATION-MATRIX-NATIONAL.csv, rows NAT-ALL-*).
- Home-based work: WA guidance says home-based operators must contact local government before starting (NAT-HAIR-WA-03, read via matrix). Other states' home-based rules are UNCONFIRMED.
- Privacy: the OAIC states that a business with annual turnover of 3 million dollars or less is generally exempt from the Privacy Act. A small business is still covered if it is a health service provider, trades in personal information, or meets other listed tests (read, 5 Sep 2024). UNCONFIRMED: whether massage, fitness or grooming businesses count as health service providers. Check before publishing any lesson that says a small business is exempt.
- Spam law: the Spam Act conditions are in section 9.

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** structure choice; the ABN obligation test; state licence lists beyond the matrix sample; trades-specific registrations (trades matrices).

## 2. Quoting and pricing for profit

**What the lesson should cover:** how to build a quote from a cost base, how to show the total price, and how to avoid misleading or unclear pricing.

- Total price: the ACCC says the displayed total must include taxes, duties and all unavoidable or pre-selected extra fees. Optional charges that are not pre-selected need not be included (read, 1 Oct 2026).
- Card surcharges: the ACCC page says changes to card surcharge rules start on 1 October 2026. The details were not read. UNCONFIRMED for lesson content.
- Service standard: the ACCC personal services guide says services must be done with due care and skill, be fit for the consumer's stated purpose, and be supplied in a reasonable time if no time is set. It also says pricing and terms must be explained before the sale (read; copyright 2013). Check against current ACL guidance.
- Remedies: under the same guide, a minor failure must be given a free, timely chance to fix. A major failure can lead to a refund or compensation (read; 2013).
- Labour cost: award rates for the sector are the starting point for labour cost (see section 10). Use the current award pay guide for the year, not a figure from any lesson.
- No industry price or margin source was found. Any pricing formula in a lesson must be labelled as a method, not a benchmark.

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** whether the 2013 personal services guide remains the current ACCC position; card surcharge rule details; any pricing benchmark.

## 3. Premium jobs and specialist credentials

**What the lesson should cover:** how higher-paying work (lesson type RH) is earned through skill, documented competence and correct licensing, without claiming a licence the business does not hold.

- Training packages used in the personal briefs: SHB30516 (barbering), SIS30321 (fitness), ACM30122 (animal care, with pet grooming as a specialisation), HLT42021 (massage). Each is marked Approved on the training.gov.au PDF. Current status on the live record was not confirmed (see each brief).
- A qualification is not a licence unless the regulator says so. The matrix found licences only in specific places: for example, Queensland higher-risk personal appearance services, ACT boarding under Part 3A, and council permits for public land. Lessons must say which one applies, if any.
- Representations: the ACCC guide says a claim about a service is misleading if it is untrue or incomplete, and that intent does not matter (2021). So a credential claim must be accurate and must not imply a licence.
- Premium work is not a reason to go into a higher-risk line. Injectables are excluded from mobile beauty (DECISIONS.md). Higher-risk services in Queensland need a licence (MBEAU-QLD-01, read via matrix).

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** the beauty training package code; whether Pilates and dog-walking credentials have a national standard; the Queensland competency condition HLTINF005 (see beauty.md).

## 4. Upselling and add-on services

**What the lesson should cover:** how to offer add-ons that help the customer and stay inside the business's scope, with the total price shown and no pressure.

- Add-ons must be shown in the total price if pre-selected or unavoidable (ACCC price displays, 1 Oct 2026).
- The ACCC small business page says a business must not use signs such as "no refunds" to remove consumer rights. It also says a business must not imply that an extended warranty is needed to keep basic rights (read, 7 Oct 2026).
- The ACCC 2021 guide says a business must not act unconscionably toward consumers. Pressure selling can cross that line. Courts look at bargaining strength and good faith (read, 2021).
- An add-on that falls in a licensed or higher-risk line needs the licence first. Example: a higher-risk treatment in Queensland (see section 3).

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** the points at which pressure selling becomes unconscionable in a home-service setting; ACL rules for add-on warranties in each trade.

## 5. Partner referrals and commission

**What the lesson should cover:** how a referral arrangement is set up, how it is disclosed, and how it is taxed. It should also warn about schemes that reward customers for sending other customers.

- Tax: the ATO says commissions received as part of business activities are assessable income. Its page covers sales commissions and commissions for online promotional work. It does not name referral fees, so the general business income rule would apply (read, 1 Oct 2026). Confirm for trade referral fees.
- Referral selling: the ACCC 2021 guide says referral selling is illegal in the consumer context, where a benefit depends on sales to others. It also says pyramid schemes are prohibited (read, 2021). Whether a business-to-business commission falls under this rule is UNCONFIRMED. Do not describe a customer referral reward in any lesson without legal review.
- Disclosure: the ACCC's online review guidance says a business must not hide commercial relationships with review platforms where they could affect ratings (read, 15 Jul 2026). The same reasoning is the safest guide for disclosing commission arrangements to customers, but the legal duty for referral commissions is UNCONFIRMED.
- Property manager partners (DECISIONS.md, module inside cleaners, gardeners and handymen): any commission from a property manager is UNCONFIRMED. Real estate and property-management licensing is state-based and was not researched in this pass.
- Contract: no source was found for referral agreement terms. Lessons must not give template terms as legal advice.

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** referral fee tax treatment; whether business-to-business commission is referral selling; property manager licensing; required written terms.

## 6. Marketing and reviews

**What the lesson should cover:** truthful marketing, honest review requests, and lawful messaging.

- Reviews: the ACCC says a business must not write or arrange fake or misleading reviews, must not have relatives or paid people write reviews without disclosure, must not suppress or remove genuine negative reviews, and must not offer incentives that depend on a positive review without disclosure (read, 15 Jul 2026).
- Messaging: the ACMA says commercial messages need consent (express or inferred), the sender must be identified with accurate contact details, and an unsubscribe must be free, working, honoured within five working days, and working for at least 30 days. The sender must be able to prove consent (read; no date shown).
- Pricing claims in ads: the ACCC price display rule applies to advertising prices (section 2).
- Social media and influencers: the ACCC social media promotions page (located only) is UNCONFIRMED for content.
- Google, Facebook and other platform rules: not researched.

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** the platform rules; influencer disclosure duties beyond the ACCC page; state advertising rules for licensed services.

## 7. Bookkeeping and cash flow

**What the lesson should cover:** the records the business must keep, the tax obligations that follow from registration, and how to plan cash for tax and payroll.

- Records: the ATO says most business records must be kept for five years. Records include money received and spent, and choices, estimates and calculations used for tax and super (read, 18 Jun 2026).
- GST: the ATO says GST registration is mandatory at a current or projected GST turnover of 75,000 dollars or more, measured over a rolling twelve months. Registration is optional below that. Once voluntarily registered, the business generally stays registered for at least 12 months. Non-profits have a different threshold (read, 14 Sep 2026). Re-check the figure before each publication.
- PAYG instalments: the ATO says instalments can start automatically from the last tax return or voluntarily. They are usually quarterly. Amounts paid are offset against the tax on the return when lodged (read, 17 Dec 2024).
- PAYG withholding: a business must register before its first payment that it needs to withhold from, even if it withholds nothing (read, 24 Jul 2025 page). The ATO page lists this with digital ID setup, STP-enabled payroll software, super fund choice, FBT and state payroll tax checks.
- Payroll tax: thresholds are state-based. The ATO says to check state or territory payroll tax (read, 24 Jul 2025). Thresholds were not read. UNCONFIRMED for lesson content.
- Cash flow method: no source was found for cash-flow forecasting methods. Any method is a teaching method, not a regulated requirement.

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** payroll tax thresholds by state; GST cash or accrual accounting options; Simple BAS details; any cash-flow benchmark.

## 8. Cost control

**What the lesson should cover:** how to lower costs without removing legal duties.

- Safety duties are not optional costs. The national model position requires a register of hazardous chemicals and a current SDS for each (NAT-CHEM-01, read via matrix). Cutting SDS or training to save money breaches the duty as described in the model position.
- Workers compensation: some states exempt small employers from holding a policy but still require injury reporting. For example, NSW exempts employers paying 7,500 dollars or less in annual wages with no apprentices or trainees, but injuries must still be notified (NAT-ALL-NSW-01, read via matrix). SA requires injuries to be reported and registered even where the registration threshold is not met (NAT-ALL-SA-01). Do not teach the exemption as a way to avoid the duty to report.
- Award pay: pay below the award rate is not a saving. Fair Work says pay slips must be issued within one working day of payday and that records of pay rates and hours must be kept (read, Fair Work small business page).
- Vehicle and fuel deductions: the ATO rules for vehicle expenses were not read. UNCONFIRMED for lesson content.

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** vehicle deduction rules; equipment depreciation rules; insurance pricing.

## 9. Automation and scheduling

**What the lesson should cover:** using booking software and automated messages without breaching privacy or spam rules, and keeping records that the ATO requires.

- Privacy: see section 1 for the OAIC small business position. Booking data about a customer's pets, home access or health may be personal information. Whether a business is covered depends on turnover and on whether it is a health service provider (UNCONFIRMED for this set of businesses).
- Messaging: automated reminders that promote a service are commercial messages and need consent, identification and an unsubscribe (ACMA, read). Whether a booking confirmation counts as a commercial message is UNCONFIRMED: the ACMA page read does not address it. Until checked, treat any reminder that also promotes a service as commercial.
- Records: an automated booking system must still keep records for five years (ATO, section 7).
- Scheduling limits from councils: council permits set site hours. Examples from the matrix: Stonnington's permit runs training between 6am and 9pm, Holdfast Bay's between 6am and 8pm, and Ryde's dog minding permit has set activity hours (read via matrix). Scheduling must respect the hours on the permit.
- AI tools and automated decisions: no source was found in this pass. UNCONFIRMED.

## 10. Hiring and supervision

**What the lesson should cover:** when someone is an employee, what the employer must do before the first payment, and how to supervise safely.

- Before the first worker: the ATO lists setting up digital ID and linking it to the business through Relationship Authorisation Manager, using STP-enabled payroll software, registering for PAYG withholding before the first withholding payment, choosing a default super fund, checking FBT and checking state payroll tax (read, 24 Jul 2025).
- Employee or contractor: the business.gov.au page says contractors run their own business and sell services to others. Employees work within another business that controls how, where and when the work is done. No single factor decides it, and an ABN or an invoice does not settle the question alone. Wrongly calling an employee a contractor is illegal (read, 25 Aug 2025 metadata).
- Pay and records: pay slips within one working day of payday, records of pay rates and hours, and super paid at least every three months (Fair Work small business page, read; no date shown).
- Awards: the Hair and Beauty Industry Award 2020 covers hairdressing, massage, waxing and nail services, but does not name mobile services (MA000005, read). The Fitness Industry Award 2020 covers fitness-industry employers (MA000094, read). The Animal Care and Veterinary Services Award 2020 covers vets and animal-care charities and does not name grooming, boarding, dog walking or pet sitting (MA000118, read). The Fair Work animal attendants page names the Miscellaneous Award for pet boarding businesses (read). Coverage for grooming, dog walking, pet sitting and yoga or Pilates is UNCONFIRMED.
- Minimum wage: do not state a rate from this brief. The Fair Work page's 2023 figure is outdated.
- Supervision and WHS: WorkSafe Victoria says employers must ensure their work does not expose others to risk, and that self-employed people must do the same so far as reasonably practicable (matrix ALL-VIC-01, reviewed 3 Apr 2025 page). Other states' supervision duties: UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
- Workers compensation: employer obligations by state are in the matrix (NAT-ALL-*).
- Child-related checks: Working with Children Check rules apply in Victoria only where the category fits (vic.gov.au page, updated 12 Oct 2026, read via matrix).

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** award coverage for each personal and pet business; contractor rules for sole trainers; supervision duties by state.

## 11. Rostering

**What the lesson should cover:** rostering that respects award hours, breaks and penalty rates, council time limits, and worker fatigue.

- Award rules: hours, breaks, overtime and penalty rates are set by the award that covers the worker. The award pages checked (MA000005, MA000094, MA000118) were read at summary level only. Clause-level hours and break rules were not read. UNCONFIRMED.
- Penalty rates: the Fair Work penalty rates page exists (located only, not read). UNCONFIRMED for lesson content.
- Records of hours: the Fair Work small business page says records of hours worked must be kept (read).
- Council time limits: Stonnington's permit sets training hours (6am to 9pm); Holdfast Bay sets 6am to 8pm; Ryde's dog minding permit has set hours (read via matrix). A roster for a permitted activity must stay inside those hours.
- Fatigue and hours in WHS: not researched in this pass. UNCONFIRMED.

**Unconfirmed in this topic:** clause-level award hours; penalty rate values (do not state any value from this brief); state fatigue rules.

- Course slug: `bond`
- Group: Cleaning and hygiene

- Brief date: 2026-11-02
- Status: DRAFT. Reviewer status: NOT REVIEWED. Not verified, not published.

## 1. Scope and boundary

- Cleaning of rental premises when a tenancy ends, to a standard set by the tenancy agreement, the tenancy law and the condition report.
- Kitchen and bathroom lessons are shared with residential, commercial and strata (DECISIONS.md).
- Out of scope: biohazard, mould, hoarding, fire and smoke, flood, and deceased-estate clean-outs unless placed in the High-risk cleaning module.
- Out of scope: any claim that a clean will secure a full bond refund. The outcome is decided by the landlord, agent, tribunal or the tenancy law.

## 2. Key methods and standards (Australia)

- Cleaning method (room by room, top to bottom, fixtures and appliances, carpets and hard floors): general practice. No Australian method standard confirmed for bond cleans. UNCONFIRMED.
- Benchmark for "clean": tenancy law sets the standard by reference to how the premises were at the start of the tenancy, allowing for fair wear and tear. NSW, READ: tenants must keep the property reasonably clean, and leave it close to its starting condition, excluding fair wear and tear. Routine tasks such as cleaning windows, dusting and cobwebs are the tenant's responsibility.
- Victoria: Consumer Affairs Victoria Guideline 2 on cleanliness is LISTED. Its text was not read this pass (the document returned binary content). Its status as guidance, and what it says about carpets, ovens and bathrooms: UNCONFIRMED.
- Condition reports: the entry and exit condition report is the main evidence in disputes. Form and rules by state: UNCONFIRMED.
- Chemical and equipment rules: as residential brief (SDS, AS/NZS 3760 LISTED).
- Carpets in a bond clean: see the carpet and upholstery brief (AS/NZS 3733 is the carpet maintenance standard named in trade press).

## 3. Main hazards

- Chemicals used in strong cleaning (oven, grout and mould-adjacent products). Ventilation and SDS required. Mould itself is out of scope.
- Falls from ladders when cleaning high fittings, ceiling fans, range hoods and windows. See the window brief for height rules (READ, SafeWork NSW construction page).
- Slips in wet bathrooms and kitchens.
- Manual handling of appliances and furniture (LISTED code).
- Unknown materials in older homes. Possible asbestos-containing materials: stop and refer under the asbestos code (READ, model status only).
- Inspection of vacant properties: unknown hazards such as pests, animal waste and damage. UNCONFIRMED.

## 5. Typical scope boundaries

- Do not promise a bond refund, a passed inspection or a certificate.
- Do not decide what counts as fair wear and tear, or make a tenancy determination. Refer to the agent or the tribunal.
- Do not do repairs (paint, carpet repair, trade work). Report damage in writing.
- Stop and refer any suspected mould, pest, biohazard or asbestos. Do not clean over it.

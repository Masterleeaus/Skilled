## 1. Scope and boundaries

In scope:
- Mowing, edging, blade care, and clippings management.
- Aeration, dethatching and top-dressing (soil-based products to a standard named below).
- Basic fertilising and watering advice, and the client's own product use.
- Identification of common lawn issues for referral (pests, fungal disease, weeds, drought stress). Diagnosis beyond visual checks UNCONFIRMED.
- Seasonal calendars and recurring schedules (CATALOGUE-V2 NEW-LAW-04).

Out of scope or referred:
- Applying herbicides or pesticides for hire unless the state rule permits it (section 5: NSW pesticide licences).
- Moving turf, soil, mulch or potted plants out of fire ant biosecurity zones without the required steps (section 5).
- Tree work, hedge removal at height, and any structural or drainage works.
- Irrigation repairs (see `irrigation.md`), electrical and plumbing work.
- Waste removal of asbestos or other hazardous materials found in soil. Refer to a licensed removalist (section 5).

## 2. Key technical methods

- Mowing: set the mowing height for the turf species and season; mow when the grass is dry; do not remove more than the height rule the species allows. Species-specific heights UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
- Blade sharpness and mower setup checks before each job.
- Edging with a method that avoids damage to paths and garden edges.
- Aeration: core or spike at the season the species prefers; record depth and spacing. Specific timings UNCONFIRMED.
- Dethatching and top-dressing: use a soil product that meets AS 4419 (soils for landscaping and garden use), per the listing below.
- Fertiliser and weed product use: follow the product label directions and safety data sheet; record product, rate and area.
- Watering advice: align with local water restrictions (UNCONFIRMED by state in this pass).
- Records: each visit, area, equipment, products, weather, and client sign-off.

## 4. Main hazards

- Powered equipment: blade contact, thrown objects (stones, debris), noise and vibration (hearing and hand-arm exposure), and ride-on rollover (specific rules UNCONFIRMED).
- Chemical exposure from fertilisers and weed products; drift; storage and spills.
- Heat, UV and dehydration on long mowing jobs.
- Manual handling of bags, soil and turf rolls.
- Underground services when aerating or edging: check with Before You Dig Australia (free referral, read; no date) before any excavation. Aeration and edging usually do not dig deep, but the course should teach the check.
- Electrical hazards from cords and electric mowers near wet grass or water.
- Biosecurity: spreading soil, turf or mulch into fire ant zones, or across state lines.
- Plant allergens and insects: specific hazards UNCONFIRMED in this pass.

## 5. Regulator references and licence facts

Pesticide and herbicide applications for hire:

- [NSW, pest management technician licence] Read on the NSW Service page (last published 11 February 2026). The licence allows a person to apply pesticides other than fumigants for a fee or reward or for a public authority; it is valid for 5 years. The page does not mention herbicides or gardens. Whether a lawn herbicide job for a fee needs this licence is UNCONFIRMED.
- [NSW, ground applicator licence] NSW EPA fact sheet (August 2018, EPA 2018P1066, read): from 1 July 2018 an individual who sprays weeds for a fee needs a ground applicator licence (valid 5 years). Amenity horticulture, including gardens and lawns, is listed as not needing it. Contract weed spraying on private land needs it.
- Other states: no matrix rows. UNCONFIRMED in this pass.

Biosecurity:

- Fire ant biosecurity zones (Queensland and NSW, read; undated): soil, hay, mulch, manure, quarry materials, turf and potted plants have movement restrictions. Anyone living or working in a zone who buys or sells these must follow the Biosecurity Regulation 2016 steps or apply for a biosecurity instrument permit. Interstate movement may need a biosecurity certificate. NSW has temporarily suspended turf movements from South East Queensland (per the page).

Asbestos (relevant to soil and debris near old structures):

- [QLD-10, CONFIRMED] Class A and Class B licences; Class A any amount of friable asbestos and more than 10 m2 of non-friable; Class B more than 10 m2 of non-friable.
- [VIC-32, CONFIRMED] Unlicensed non-friable removal allowed only up to 10 m2 in total and one hour in any seven days (OHS Regulations 2017).
- [NSW-15, PARTIAL] Class A and B names confirmed; area thresholds UNCONFIRMED.
- [WA-26, CONFIRMED] Class B for more than 10 m2 of non-friable asbestos.
- [SA-20, CONFIRMED] Class B for more than 10 m2 of non-friable asbestos.
- [TAS-19, CONFIRMED] Class B above 10 m2; 10 m2 or less has its own rule (page).
- [NT-19, CONFIRMED] Friable any amount needs Class A; non-friable more than 10 m2 needs a licence.
- [ACT-20, PARTIAL] Class A and B scope confirmed; area thresholds not stated.

Height: boom-type elevating work platform (WP) licences apply at boom length 11 m or more in NSW, QLD, WA, TAS, ACT and NT [NSW-16, QLD-11, WA-18, TAS-20, ACT-22, NT-20, all CONFIRMED]. Scissor lifts are not named as licence classes in NSW, QLD or ACT (matrix PARTIAL or UNCONFIRMED), and are stated as not needing a licence in VIC [VIC-22, CONFIRMED] and NT [NT-21, CONFIRMED]. Scissor lifts in WA [WA-19], TAS [TAS-21] and ACT [ACT-23] are UNCONFIRMED.

Underground services:

- Before You Dig Australia (BYDA) is the free pre-excavation referral service (read; no date). Requests are made through an online portal with a free account. Asset owners respond directly with plans.

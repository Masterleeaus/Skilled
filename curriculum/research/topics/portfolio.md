## Key rules for this brief

- This is the highest licence-risk course in this batch. Managing property for other people, including rent collection, tenancy and trust money, is licensed or regulated in several states. The lesson content must not teach or imply unlicensed letting or managing.
- No matrix rows cover real estate or property management licences. Facts below come from the state pages named in section 8, with their status labels.
- Source lessons: the portfolio content comes from the specialist set C19-L01, L02, L03, L05 and L06 (no C19-L04 in `planning/CATALOGUE-V2.csv`), all marked Group and Rewrite. C19-L03 (vacate and tenancy scope) is flagged for a licence notice.

## 1. Scope and boundaries

In scope (operational support for the portfolio, within the licence position in section 5):
- Portfolio register: addresses, owners, keys and access records, authority documents, and property-specific checklists.
- Condition evidence: dated photos and condition notes that separate pre-existing damage from cleaning or maintenance defects (C19-L02).
- Service planning: maintenance and clean schedules, contractor coordination, and cost records (C19-L05).
- Handover notes to the agent or owner: factual completion notes and a rework plan (C19-L06).
- Client and contractor records, and privacy-safe handling of tenant and owner data.

Out of scope or restricted (must not be taught as an operator task):
- Acting as a property manager, leasing agent or managing agent without the state licence (section 5 gives the state rows).
- Signing tenancy agreements, giving legal tenancy advice, serving notices, or deciding on bond or rent matters.
- Holding or handling rent or bond money in a trust account unless the licence allows it.
- Advertising or marketing properties for lease or sale without the licence.
- Lock changes and key handling beyond the record-keeping rules (see `locksmith.md`).
- High-hazard property conditions (biohazard, hoarding, crime scene, drug contamination, fire or flood damage): restricted-work triage applies; refer.

## 2. Key technical methods

- Register build: one record per property with a unique ID, owner details (privacy-restricted), access method (recorded, not stored in plain text), and document references.
- Condition report method: entry and exit photos from fixed positions; date stamp; written condition notes against the property checklist; separate lists for pre-existing damage and new damage.
- Maintenance scheduling: recurring jobs by season and by asset age; approval thresholds written into the owner's instructions.
- Contractor management: verify licence and insurance details from the official licence registers before the job (UNCONFIRMED register names per state in this pass); keep certificates on file.
- Cost tracking: job cost, variation approval, and monthly summary to the owner or agent.
- Document control: retention periods UNCONFIRMED per state; apply the owner's and the licence holder's rules.
- Reporting: monthly or per-job reports stating facts, with no legal opinion.

## 4. Main hazards

- Physical: site visits to older or damaged properties (falls, trips, sharp edges, unstable structures, pests).
- Hazardous materials in older homes: asbestos and lead (see the trade briefs; thresholds in section 5 of `handyman.md`).
- Personal safety when attending properties alone, including occupied tenancies and unknown persons.
- Data and privacy: tenant records, identity documents, key codes, and photos may hold personal information.
- Financial risk: handling money without a licence or trust account.
- Consumer risk: inaccurate reporting or condition notes that lead to disputes.

## 5. Regulator references and licence facts

NSW:
- [NSW, becoming a property agent page, published 27 May 2025, modified 22 Sep 2025; read] Licence types named: real estate agent; strata managing agent; stock and station agent; assistant agent certificate of registration; auctioneer accreditation; dual licence; corporation licence. The page does not name residential rental management as a licensable activity.
- Whether residential rental management needs a licence in NSW: UNCONFIRMED in this pass.
- The NSW property management page (read) sets out managing agent duties, trust money for short-term rental (under the Property and Stock Agents Act 2002), and complaints handling; it does not state a licence requirement for rental management.

VIC:
- [VIC estate agent licence, ABLIS page, read; no date] Licence is needed to buy, sell, lease or manage real estate or businesses on behalf of any other person (examples include landlord and tenant). Rental property management is not named as its own category; it falls under "manage real estate" on behalf of others. Administered by the Business Licensing Authority. Individuals must be 18 or older and hold the prescribed course or equivalent qualifications, or have held a Victorian licence in the last five years. Corporations must have a licensed estate agent as officer in effective control. Status for residential rental management: PARTIAL.

QLD:
- [QLD Office of Fair Trading statement, read; undated] A resident letting agent licence allows a person to rent out and manage units in a building complex, and also to collect rent and operate a trust account for residents and owners. The statement reports an unlicensed resident letting agent who acted without a licence and used a trust account without one. Status for other property management: UNCONFIRMED in this pass; the Property Occupations Act 2014 is named as the governing Act (search result and statement).

WA, SA, TAS, ACT, NT:
- UNCONFIRMED in this pass. The matrices do not cover property agent licences. Do not state a position for these states.

Other rules in this course:
- Trust money: the NSW page says payments for short-term rental accommodation are treated as trust money under the Property and Stock Agents Act 2002 (read).
- Privacy: see section 3.
